# Remote AI Job Application Checklist

**Track:** career-prep
**Source:** LinkedIn job ad — 100% remote AI Engineer, 2+ years mandatory, 70 LPA fixed (shortlink coverage)
**Status:** `review`
**Hand-written:** yes — seed note; sets the convention for later `career-prep/` lanes

---

## Framing

### Why this note exists
Strong candidates lose remote AI roles on process, not skill: a dead apply link, a cold application into a pile of 500, negotiating base against a fixed band. This checklist covers the three mechanical stages — apply hygiene, referral flow, and CTC negotiation — calibrated to the source ad (100% remote, 70 LPA fixed, 2+ years mandatory). Read the companion [JD-to-bar note](jd-to-bar-remote-ai-roles.md) first; this one assumes you already understand the bar.

### What it tests (the meta-skill)
Whether you treat the job search as a pipeline with controllable conversion at each stage, the same way you would treat any production system: measure, fix the biggest drop-off first.

---

## The checklist

### 1. Apply-link hygiene (before you touch anything else)
- [ ] Resolve the actual apply link yourself — short links rot. Confirm it loads (HTTP 200, real form, not a login wall or a deleted post) before investing hours in a take-home.
- [ ] Check the post date and comment count. A weeks-old viral post (100+ comments) means the funnel is saturated — route through referral (stage 2), not the public link.
- [ ] Save the full JD text locally. LinkedIn posts get edited or deleted; you need the original expectations to map against the [JD-to-bar note](jd-to-bar-remote-ai-roles.md).
- [ ] Verify the "remote" claim: remote-first (async culture, timezone overlap stated) vs remote-tolerant (one remote req, HQ-centric process). Ask the recruiter directly which it is — it changes the interview (async writing samples vs whiteboard).

### 2. Referral flow (highest conversion lever — do this first)
- [ ] Find the hiring manager or an IC on the team, not just any employee. A referral from the team carries multiples of a generic employee referral.
- [ ] Your ask message is three lines: who you are (one line, with the metric), why this team (one line, specific — cite their stack or problem), the ask (one line: "would you be comfortable referring me for [req link]?"). Attach nothing unless asked; link your profile.
- [ ] Give the referrer ammunition: a 3-bullet summary of your fit mapped to the ad's expectations, so their internal note writes itself.
- [ ] If no warm path exists, create one: a substantive comment or question on the hiring manager's public post beats a cold DM. Contribute first, ask second.
- [ ] Track your funnel: applied → screen → take-home → onsite → offer. If screens convert but take-homes do not, the fix is the take-home rubric (companion note), not more applications.

### 3. CTC negotiation at a fixed band (70 LPA fixed)
- [ ] Read "fixed" literally: base is set. Your negotiation surface is joining bonus, ESOPs/refreshers, remote-setup and learning allowances, and review-cycle timing (an early first review is worth real money at this band).
- [ ] Get the split in writing before the final round: fixed vs variable vs ESOP strike price and vesting schedule. "70 LPA fixed" should mean exactly that — confirm there is no variable hiding inside the number.
- [ ] Never disclose your current CTC first. Anchor on the stated band ("the ad states 70 fixed; I am evaluating against that") and let them confirm fit.
- [ ] Competing offers are the only real leverage on a fixed band — parallelize applications so timelines overlap. Two final rounds in the same fortnight beats any argument.
- [ ] Remote-specific terms to confirm: timezone expectations and overlap hours, travel requirements (onsite weeks per quarter?), equipment/internet allowances, and whether compensation is location-adjusted now or later.

### 4. Timeline discipline
- [ ] Reply to recruiter emails within 24 hours; remote pipelines deprioritize slow candidates silently.
- [ ] Timebox the take-home to one focused weekend. Past that, returns collapse and employed candidates burn out — a good rubric rewards a tight, honest submission over a sprawling one.
- [ ] After each stage, send a 3-line thank-you restating one thing you learned about the team. It is a writing sample for an async-remote culture, not politeness theater.

## Pitfalls

- **Cold-applying to a viral post and waiting** — **Better:** saturated funnels convert near zero; spend the same hour finding one team member to talk to. Referral first, public link as fallback.
- **Negotiating base against a stated-fixed number** — **Better:** accept the fixed figure as set and negotiate everything around it (bonus, equity, review timing, remote terms). Pushing base signals you did not read the ad.
- **Accepting "70 LPA" without the split in writing** — **Better:** fixed/variable/ESOP strike/vesting confirmed by email before the final round. Verbal band statements drift; written ones do not.
- **Treating remote logistics as small print** — **Better:** overlap hours, travel, and location-adjustment clauses change the real value of the offer by lakhs. Confirm them with the same seriousness as the CTC split.
