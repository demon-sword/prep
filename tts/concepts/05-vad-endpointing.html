05Voice Activity Detection and Endpointing

Deciding when speech starts and stops — energy and zero-crossing baselines against neural VAD, and the asymmetric cost that defines the tuning: a premature endpoint cuts the caller off mid-sentence, a late one leaves dead air that reads as the agent being broken.

Theory

Two different jobs that get called one name

VAD is a per-frame classifier: given 10–30 ms of audio, is this speech. Endpointing is a state machine on top of that trace deciding the caller's turn is over, commit and go. Conflate them and you get an agent with excellent frame accuracy that still interrupts every caller who pauses to read a member ID off a card.

The classical detector is two statistics per frame. Short-time energy in dBFS, 20·log₁₀(rms), separates voiced speech from silence — a vowel near −25 dBFS against a quiet line's −55 dBFS floor. Zero-crossing rate, the fraction of adjacent samples changing sign, catches what energy cannot: an unvoiced fricative like /s/ sits well below a vowel in level but crosses zero constantly. It breaks on a phone line, because broadband noise also has a high ZCR — what rescues fricatives in a quiet office fires on clinic ventilation. Add a tracked noise floor and hysteresis and you have roughly WebRTC's GMM VAD (10/20/30 ms frames, 8–48 kHz, four aggressiveness modes). Silero-class neural VADs learn the spectro-temporal shape of speech rather than thresholding loudness — that is what buys robustness to babble and hold music — on fixed 32 ms chunks (512 samples at 16 kHz, 256 at 8 kHz).

The endpointer is where your milliseconds go

Three knobs: minimum speech duration (~100–200 ms) before onset, so a cough or a DTMF tone does not start a turn; hangover, the trailing silence required to declare the end; and maximum utterance length, the escape hatch for when the VAD never goes quiet. Hangover sits on the critical path of every turn. Budget one against the ~800 ms at which a caller reads the pause as dead air: 600 ms hangover + 100 ms ASR finalisation + 350 ms LLM time-to-first-token + 150 ms TTS time-to-first-audio + ~80 ms for both network legs = 1280 ms, of which hangover is 47%. At 250 ms the turn lands at 930 ms. So why not 100? Because what you are thresholding is the caller's own intra-utterance pauses, and hesitations of 400–800 ms are ordinary — "my member ID is … four seven two …". Every hangover value is a percentile cut on that distribution, and the cut is a false cut-off rate.

The asymmetry, and the failure modes

The errors do not cost the same. Waiting 300 ms too long costs 300 ms; cutting a caller off costs the truncated turn, the answer to half a sentence, and the repair — seconds. So the minimum of E = hangover + p_cut·C_repair sits well slow of where a latency dashboard alone would put it. Semantic endpointing buys the difference back: score whether the partial transcript is syntactically complete, then take a short hangover after "…nineteen sixty" and a long one after "my date of birth is".

Three characteristic failures. Premature endpoint: the agent answers over the caller's third digit. Never endpoints: a noisy clinic or hold music holds the VAD hot, no silence run reaches hangover, and the turn ends only at the max-utterance timeout — fifteen seconds of dead air. Self-trigger: the agent's own TTS leaks back from the far end and fires the inbound VAD, so it barges in on itself; echo cancellation runs before the VAD, not after. 

# Frame features + endpoint state machine. 20 ms = 160 samples @ 8 kHz.
FRAME_MS, HANGOVER_MS, MIN_SPEECH_MS, MAX_UTT_MS = 20, 600, 140, 15000

def energy_dbfs(x):     # frame in [-1, 1]
    return 20 * math.log10(math.sqrt((x * x).mean()) + 1e-12)

def zcr(x):             # unvoiced /s/ ~0.35, a vowel ~0.05
    return (np.diff(np.sign(x)) != 0).mean()

def is_speech(x, thresh_db):
    db = energy_dbfs(x)
    return db > thresh_db or (db > thresh_db - 10 and zcr(x) > 0.28)

def endpointer(frames, thresh_db):
    state, run, sil = "silence", 0, 0
    for i, x in enumerate(frames):
        t, v = (i + 1) * FRAME_MS, is_speech(x, thresh_db)
        if state == "silence":
            run = run + FRAME_MS if v else 0
            if run >= MIN_SPEECH_MS: state = "speech"   # rejects coughs/DTMF
        else:
            sil = 0 if v else sil + FRAME_MS
            if sil >= HANGOVER_MS: return t             # commit the turn
        if t >= MAX_UTT_MS: return t                    # VAD never went quiet
The VAD trace is not audio, and that matters. Tuning needs thousands of real calls, which are PHI. Store per-frame energy, ZCR, speech probability and segment boundaries — all the sweep below consumes — and that corpus stays off the recording retention clock. Hosted VAD ships PHI off-box under a BAA; a Silero-class model runs in-process on the media server, inside the boundary.

The line I'd actually say

"I'd separate the two things people call VAD. Frame-level detection is nearly solved — a Silero-class neural VAD on 32 ms chunks beats energy plus zero-crossing on anything with background noise, because energy thresholding fails on exactly the frames that matter: unvoiced fricatives are quiet, and clinic noise is loud. The hard part is the endpointer on top, and specifically the hangover, because that sits on the critical path of every turn. At 600 ms it's about half of a 1280 ms response — the single biggest and cheapest line item in the budget."

"But you can't just shrink it, because the distribution you're cutting against is the caller's own hesitation pauses, and 400 to 800 ms is normal when someone is reading an ID off a card. The costs are asymmetric: waiting 300 ms too long costs 300 ms, cutting someone off costs the repair turn — several seconds. So I'd tune on expected cost per turn = hangover + p_cut × repair cost, measured on real call pause distributions, not on the latency dashboard alone. Then I'd buy the difference back with semantic endpointing — short hangover when the partial transcript is syntactically complete, long when it dangles. And I'd check echo cancellation runs before the VAD, or the agent trips its own detector on its own TTS."

Takeaways

  • VAD is a frame classifier; endpointing is the state machine above it. Onset needs a minimum speech duration (~100–200 ms) to reject coughs and DTMF, offset needs hangover, and you need a max-utterance escape hatch. Near-perfect frame accuracy still gives a terrible agent if the hangover is wrong.
  • Hangover is the biggest line in the turn budget. 600 ms hangover + 100 ms ASR finalisation + 350 ms LLM TTFT + 150 ms TTS TTFA + ~80 ms network = 1280 ms, against roughly 800 ms before the caller hears dead air. Nothing else is as cheap to cut — or as expensive to cut wrong.
  • Tune on asymmetric cost, not accuracy. E = hangover + p_cut · C_repair; a repair turn costs seconds, so the optimum sits well slow of where a latency percentile alone would put it. Semantic endpointing is the escape from one constant: short hangover on a syntactically complete partial, long on a dangling one.
  • Energy plus ZCR is the baseline that breaks on a phone line. Unvoiced fricatives sit well below vowels in level, and the high-ZCR rule that rescues them also fires on broadband noise. WebRTC's GMM VAD is the classic floor; a Silero-class neural VAD — a small model distributed as ONNX and trainable in PyTorch, on fixed 512-sample/16 kHz or 256-sample/8 kHz chunks — buys robustness for ~32 ms of granularity plus inference you should benchmark yourself.
  • Name the three failure modes. Premature endpoint (the agent talks over digit three); never-endpoints (noise holds the VAD hot to the max-utterance timeout, so the caller gets fifteen seconds of nothing); self-trigger (the agent's own TTS echoes back and fires the inbound VAD). Echo cancellation belongs before the VAD. Carrier DTX matters too: with silence suppression on, "silence" arrives as comfort-noise frames rather than a quiet signal.
  • Keep the tuning corpus as traces, not audio. Per-frame energy, ZCR, speech probability and segment boundaries re-run the whole sweep and are not recorded voice; export cut-off rate and p95 endpoint latency to Prometheus and put the operating curve in Grafana. Hosted VAD means shipping PHI off-box under a BAA — an in-process model on the media server usually avoids the question.
