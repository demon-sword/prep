09Forced Alignment

Recovering which audio frames correspond to which phonemes when you have the transcript but no timings — Viterbi over an HMM chain, CTC-based alignment, and Montreal Forced Aligner in practice. The labelling step every duration predictor is trained on, and the one that fails without telling you.

Theory

Forced, meaning the transcript is not in question

An ASR decoder searches every word sequence its language model allows. A forced aligner searches exactly one: you already have the transcript, and the only unknown is when each phone was spoken. The search collapses from a lattice to a single left-to-right chain of states, and the answer is the highest-scoring monotonic path through it. FastSpeech-family duration targets and word-level call timestamps both come out of an aligner.

The trellis

Each phone is a small HMM: conventionally three left-to-right states with self-loops and no skip arcs — onset, steady, offset. Concatenate the transcript's phones, with silence at both ends and an optional short-pause between words, and you have S states covering T frames at the usual 10 ms shift, 25 ms window. Viterbi in the log domain, b the emission density and a the transitions:

δ_t(s) = log b_s(x_t) + max( δ_{t−1}(s) + log a_ss , δ_{t−1}(s−1) + log a_{s−1,s} )

Note what is not in that expression: any backward arc. Monotonicity is a property of the topology, not a penalty you tune. Each state has at most two predecessors, so the recursion is O(T·S) rather than a general HMM's O(T·S²); one backpointer per cell and a backtrace from the last state recovers the boundaries.

Numbers. "Call the clinic back" is 14 phones plus two silences — 48 states — and 1.27 s at a 10 ms shift is 127 frames: about 6,100 cells. The trellis is free; the cost is the acoustic model's forward pass. Alignment is an offline dataset job and never appears in a call's latency budget. The no-skip three-state topology also imposes a floor of 3 frames, so nothing shorter than 30 ms is representable — a flapped /t/ is not. Montreal Forced Aligner is the practical default: Kaldi underneath, a GMM-HMM triphone model trained on your corpus with speaker-adapted features, returning the per-utterance score you need below.

CTC alignment and learned alignment

Same recursion, different graph. Interleave blanks around the target label sequence for a 2L+1 graph, forbid the blank-skip between identical labels, and run the same recursion over a CTC model's posteriors — torchaudio.functional.forced_align. The model is already there and no lexicon is needed. The cost is peakiness: CTC puts most of its mass on blank and spikes on a frame or two per token, with nothing in the objective pushing that spike toward the token's true onset, so boundaries run late. Fine for timestamps, biased as duration labels. Modern TTS often learns alignment during training instead — monotonic alignment search in Glow-TTS and VITS is the same Viterbi against the model's own likelihood — which removes the external dependency, not the failure mode.

Onto the mel grid

Aligner and vocoder frames sit on different grids: MFA gives 10 ms boundaries, a 22.05 kHz mel with hop 256 is 11.61 ms. Durations must be non-negative integers summing to exactly the mel frame count; rounding each independently lets the sum drift and the length regulator builds a tensor of the wrong size. Round the cumulative boundaries and difference them — exact by construction.

The failure mode: it always succeeds

Alignment is forced. Give it a transcript that does not match the audio and it returns the best path through the wrong graph — silently, with plausible boundaries. Omit a word the caller said and those frames still have to be explained by something. Viterbi hands them to whichever reachable states dislike them least, and that is the neighbouring voiced phones — not the closing silence. A silence or short-pause model emits near-zero energy in every band, which makes it the worst available explanation for a vowel, so the trailing silence stays roughly its true length while one or two neighbours inflate to several times theirs. Include a word never spoken and the reverse happens: its phones pin at the 30 ms floor and squeeze the real ones. Nor is the damage confined to the corrupted span — the path that best absorbs orphaned frames often starts diverging several phones earlier, so boundaries in the matching part of the transcript move too. Neither case raises an exception; both quietly poison a duration predictor. So you gate on the score, not the alignment: mean per-frame log-likelihood of the winning path, thresholded per corpus, plus duration outliers and phones on the floor. An independent check: decode the audio freely and score edit distance against the transcript you were handed. Anything failing either goes to a review queue, not into training.

Narrowband, and why timestamps are a PHI tool

Alignment degrades where ASR degrades: fricative boundaries. The energy separating /s/ from /f/ sits mostly above 3.4 kHz, so on an 8 kHz leg the aligner is guessing a sibilant onset to within a couple of frames. Align with a model trained on the band you serve — a 16 kHz aligner pointed at upsampled mu-law is a domain mismatch that surfaces as a lower corpus mean score, not an obvious error. The compliance angle is a real tool: word-level timestamps let you redact a recording instead of destroying it — find the span where the member ID was spoken and zero exactly those samples, for which ±30 ms is ample. The alignment file is itself a timed index into protected audio, and inherits its retention and access controls. 

# Forced alignment, log domain. seq = phones of the KNOWN transcript.
# 3 states/phone, self-loop + advance only: monotonic by topology.
S = 3 * len(seq)
NEG = -1e9
d  = np.full((T, S), NEG); bp = np.zeros((T, S), np.int32)
d[0, 0] = logb(x[0], mu[0])                  # must start in state 0

for t in range(1, T):
    stay = d[t-1] + LOG_A_SELF
    adv  = np.concatenate(([NEG], d[t-1, :-1] + LOG_A_NEXT))
    take = adv > stay
    bp[t] = np.where(take, np.arange(S) - 1, np.arange(S))
    best  = np.where(take, adv, stay)
    d[t]  = np.where(best <= NEG/2, NEG, best + logb(x[t], mu))

path = np.zeros(T, np.int32); path[T-1] = S - 1   # must end in the last state
for t in range(T-1, 0, -1):
    path[t-1] = bp[t, path[t]]
mean_ll = d[T-1, S-1] / T                     # THE QA signal — gate on it

# 10 ms aligner grid -> 11.61 ms mel grid (22.05 kHz, hop 256).
# Round CUMULATIVE edges, then diff: sum == T_mel exactly.
edges_ms = np.cumsum(phone_frames) * 10.0
edges    = np.round(edges_ms / (1000.0 * 256 / 22050)).astype(int)
dur      = np.diff(np.concatenate(([0], edges)))
assert dur.min() >= 0 and dur.sum() == T_mel

The line I'd actually say

"Forced alignment is decoding with the search space collapsed to one hypothesis. You already know the words, so you build a left-to-right HMM chain from the transcript — three states per phone, self-loop plus advance, no skips — and run Viterbi in the log domain. Monotonicity falls out of the topology rather than a penalty, each state has two predecessors so it's O(T·S), and the whole thing is offline: it's a dataset labelling job, not something in the call's latency budget. It's where FastSpeech's duration targets come from, and it's how you get word-level timestamps on a recording."

"The thing I'd flag is that it is forced — it always returns an answer. Hand it a transcript missing a word and those frames get absorbed by the neighbouring voiced phones — a vowel next to the gap stretches to two or three times its real length, because a silence model is the last thing that would explain voiced audio — and the boundaries before the gap drift too. Hand it a word that wasn't spoken and those phones pin at the three-frame floor and squeeze the real ones. No exception, just quietly poisoned duration targets. So I gate on the mean per-frame log-likelihood of the winning path plus duration outliers, and cross-check with a free ASR decode's edit distance against the transcript. If I'm using CTC alignment because the model's already there, I'd also account for the peakiness — CTC boundaries run systematically late, which is fine for timestamps and not fine as duration labels."

Takeaways

  • One hypothesis, not a lattice. The transcript is known, so the graph is a single left-to-right chain and Viterbi over it is O(T·S) — two predecessors per state. 127 frames against 48 states is ~6,100 cells; the trellis is free and the acoustic model forward pass is the whole cost. This is an offline PyTorch/Kaldi dataset job, never in a call's latency path.
  • Monotonic by construction. Self-loop and advance are the only arcs, so no path can go backwards. The same three-state no-skip topology imposes a 30 ms floor per phone at a 10 ms shift — shorter real segments like a flapped /t/ are simply not representable.
  • The characteristic failure is silent success. A mismatched transcript still produces boundaries: a dropped word inflates its voiced neighbours — the low-energy silence model is the worst fit for orphaned vowel frames, so it does not soak them up — while an inserted word pins phones at the floor. Gate the corpus on mean per-frame log-likelihood plus duration outliers, cross-check with a free-decode edit distance, and export the scores as a Prometheus histogram over each alignment run so a corpus regression is visible before training is.
  • CTC alignment trades a lexicon for a timing bias. Interleaving blanks gives a 2L+1 graph you can Viterbi with the ASR model you already have, but peaky posteriors put boundaries systematically late. Fine for word timestamps, biased as duration labels for a length regulator.
  • Regrid carefully. 10 ms aligner frames into an 11.61 ms mel grid: round cumulative boundaries and difference them so durations are non-negative and sum exactly to the mel frame count. Store the aligned corpus as Parquet alongside the score so filtering is a query rather than a re-run.
  • Timestamps are a redaction tool and therefore PHI. Word boundaries let you mute the span where a member ID was spoken instead of deleting the call; ±30 ms is ample. The alignment file is a timed index into protected audio and inherits its retention and access controls.
