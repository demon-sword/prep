17HiFi-GAN

One forward pass turns a mel into a waveform, and two discriminators — one that averages the signal down, one that reshapes it into prime-period columns — argue about what is wrong with it. Each is blind to something the other sees, which is exactly why there are two.

Theory

A stack of upsamplers, and the fusion that feeds them

HiFi-GAN (Kong, Kim & Bae, NeurIPS 2020) takes an 80-bin mel at hop 256 — 86.1 frames per second at 22.05 kHz — and returns a waveform. The generator is fully convolutional: a kernel-7 input conv, four transposed-convolution stages with strides 8, 8, 2, 2 whose product is exactly the hop, channels halving 512→32, then a kernel-7 output conv and tanh. Nothing is autoregressive: the whole waveform lands in one pass.

The name comes from the multi-receptive-field fusion after each upsample: three residual blocks run in parallel on the same activation with kernel sizes 3, 7, 11 and dilations 1, 3, 5, their outputs averaged. The kernel-3 path sees sample-to-sample shape; the kernel-11 path adds 120 positions of span at that stage's resolution — at coarse stages, a whole pitch period. Averaging means the block never commits to a scale. V1 is 13.92 M parameters.

Two discriminators, because each is blind to something

Neither is spectral. The multi-scale discriminator, from MelGAN, runs three sub-networks: on raw audio, on a 4-tap average-pooled ×2 version, and on ×4 (two poolings stacked); the first uses spectral normalisation, the others weight normalisation. Average pooling is a low-pass, so the coarse branches judge envelope and continuity and go deaf near the top of the band — that 4-tap boxcar has an exact null at fs/4, so a 5.5 kHz tone is cancelled before the ×2 branch sees it.

The multi-period discriminator is the paper's contribution. For period p it reshapes a length-T waveform into a (T/p, p) matrix and convolves with kernels of size (k, 1) — width one. Column c is the waveform decimated by p at phase c: x[c], x[c+p], x[c+2p]… No kernel mixes two columns, and the periods are the primes 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, so no two branches share a sample grid. Decimation is not a low-pass — it aliases — so noise survives into every MPD column that MSD ×4 has averaged away. The six-layer stack, four layers of stride 3, reaches 647 rows down the height axis: at p = 11 that is 7,117 samples, 323 ms, for the cost of T/11 rows. Long-range periodicity — what goes wrong when a vocoder buzzes — is cheap to police from there, and the ablation that removes MPD is the one that hurts most.

The objective, and where the weight sits

Least squares, not cross-entropy: D minimises E[(D(y)−1)²] + E[D(G(x))²], G minimises E[(D(G(x))−1)²]. Above that sit feature matching — L1 between the discriminators' intermediate activations on real and generated audio, λ = 2 — and an L1 mel loss at λ = 45. That 45 dominates early and does most of the work of placing the right sounds in the right places; the adversarial terms stop it settling on the conditional median of the waveforms matching that mel (L2 would give the mean), one over-smoothed, muffled compromise across their phase realisations.

Cost, lookahead, and the phone leg

The paper reports V1 at roughly 168× real time on a V100 and barely above real time on CPU. A 232 ms streaming chunk (20 mel frames) therefore costs about 1.4 ms of GPU — the vocoder is not what makes a turn slow. Padding is the real cost: the generator is symmetric, not causal, and walking its receptive field back through the four upsamplers gives ≈ 27 mel frames ≈ 314 ms, so roughly 150 ms is future mel. Budget that lookahead, or chunk with overlap and pay in seams.

The characteristic failure is buzz on out-of-domain mel. Train on ground-truth mel, serve predicted mel from an acoustic model, and voiced segments go metallic while long vowels pick up a robotic hum — the generator is off its training manifold. The fix is fine-tuning on that model's own predicted mel, not a bigger discriminator. On a phone leg you discard the top of the band anyway: 8 kHz mu-law loses everything above 3.4 kHz, so low-pass before decimating or the vocoder's own HF artefacts alias into the passband. Training natively at 16 kHz is worth costing — 27% fewer samples to emit, Nyquist at 8 kHz not 11.025 kHz. Fine-tune on recorded caller audio and the checkpoint is PHI-derived, inheriting those recordings' consent and retention obligations.

# ---- V1 generator: mel -> waveform, one MRF per upsample stage -------------
sr, hop, n_mel = 22050, 256, 80
up_rates   = [8, 8, 2, 2]          # product = 256 = hop -> 86.13 mel frames/s
up_kernels = [16, 16, 4, 4]        # k = 2*stride
mrf_k, mrf_d = [3, 7, 11], [1, 3, 5]   # 3 ResBlocks in parallel, outputs averaged

def resblock_rf(k, dils):          # each dilation = conv(k,d) then conv(k,1)
    return sum((k - 1) * d + (k - 1) for d in dils)      # k=11 -> 120

R = 7                                          # conv_post, kernel 7
for u, ku in zip(up_rates[::-1], up_kernels[::-1]):
    R += max(resblock_rf(k, mrf_d) for k in mrf_k)       # MRF = max over kernels
    R  = (R + ku - 2) // u + 1                 # back through ConvTranspose1d
R += 6                                         # conv_pre -> R = 27 mel frames
# 27 * hop/sr = 314 ms, symmetric -> ~157 ms of FUTURE mel per centre sample.

# ---- MPD sub-discriminator, period p ---------------------------------------
PERIODS = [2, 3, 5, 7, 11]         # prime: no two branches share a grid
def mpd_branch(x, p):                          # x: (B, 1, T)
    if T % p: x = F.pad(x, (0, p - T % p), "reflect")
    x = x.view(B, 1, -1, p)                    # height T/p, width p
    for c in convs:                            # Conv2d(.., (5,1), stride=(3,1))
        x = F.leaky_relu(c(x), 0.1)            # width-1: column c is x[c::p]
    return x   # 6 layers -> 647 rows: p=2 -> 59 ms, p=11 -> 323 ms

The line I'd actually say

"HiFi-GAN's generator is the boring half — four transposed convs whose strides multiply to the hop, each followed by a multi-receptive-field block that averages parallel residual stacks with kernels 3, 7 and 11 so it never commits to a timescale. The interesting half is two discriminators with complementary blind spots. The multi-scale one average-pools, and pooling is a low-pass, so its coarse branches cannot see the top of the band at all. The multi-period one reshapes into a T/p-by-p matrix with width-one kernels, so each column is the waveform decimated by p — decimation aliases rather than smooths, and the stack down a column reaches about 320 ms at p=11 for very little compute. That reach is what polices long-term periodicity, and the ablation says it's the piece that matters."

"Serving-wise it's fast — about 168× real time on a V100, so a 230 ms chunk is around 1.4 ms and the vocoder is never why a turn feels slow. Padding is the real cost: a receptive field of roughly 27 mel frames means about 150 ms of future mel. And the failure I watch for is buzz on out-of-domain mel — train on ground truth, serve predicted mel, voiced segments go metallic. You fix it by fine-tuning the vocoder on the acoustic model's own predictions, not by adding discriminators."

Takeaways

  • Strides multiply to the hop; MRF supplies the scales. 8×8×2×2 = 256, then three parallel ResBlocks (kernels 3/7/11, dilations 1/3/5) averaged, so each stage covers sample-to-sample shape and a whole pitch period. V1 is 13.92 M parameters — the PyTorch checkpoint sits beside the acoustic model on one Triton instance.
  • MSD pools, MPD decimates — that is the whole distinction. Pooling low-passes: a 4-tap boxcar nulls fs/4 and fs/2 outright, so the pooled branches are all but deaf to hiss. Decimation aliases, so every MPD column keeps the noise variance. Neither is redundant.
  • Width-one kernels buy cheap long-range reach. (k,1) kernels never mix columns, and the six-layer stack gives 647 rows — 59 ms at p=2, 323 ms at p=11. That is where "does this still sound periodic three syllables later" gets judged.
  • λmel = 45 does the placement; the GAN terms do the texture. A pointwise mel loss alone converges to a conditional median — muffled, over-smoothed. Least-squares adversarial loss plus feature matching at λ = 2 is what stops it.
  • The failure mode is buzz from train/serve mel mismatch. Fine-tune on the acoustic model's predicted mel. Export to ONNX and gate promotion on ASR WER through the synthesised audio, alarmed in Prometheus — a buzzing vocoder surfaces as WER drift long before a ticket.
  • Budget the padding, not the FLOPs. ~168× real time is 1.4 ms per 232 ms chunk, but ~27 mel frames of receptive field is ~150 ms of lookahead. On a PSTN leg, low-pass before decimating to 8 kHz, and treat a caller-fine-tuned checkpoint as PHI-derived.
