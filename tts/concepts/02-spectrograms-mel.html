02Spectrograms and the Mel Scale

The STFT as the bridge from waveform to image — window length trading time resolution against frequency resolution, hop length setting the frame rate every downstream model inherits, and mel filterbanks compressing linear frequency onto a perceptual scale so 80 bins carry what 513 FFT bins did.

Theory

The STFT is three parameters and one compromise

A waveform is a one-dimensional list of samples; almost every speech model wants a two-dimensional time-frequency image. The short-time Fourier transform gets you there by chopping the signal into overlapping frames, multiplying each frame by a window function, and taking a DFT of each. Three parameters define it: win_length (how many samples per frame), hop_length (how far you advance between frames) and n_fft (the DFT size, which must be at least win_length and is normally the next power of two, with the difference zero-padded).

The compromise is forced by mathematics, not by implementation, and it is worth being precise about which quantity is which. The bin spacing is fs / n_fft, but the resolution — the smallest frequency separation at which two tones stay distinguishable — is set by how long you actually observed the signal, fs / win_length. Zero-padding to a larger n_fft packs the bins closer together and makes the plot smoother; it interpolates, it does not resolve. Time resolution is roughly the window duration. Multiply the two real quantities and the window length cancels: you cannot shrink both.

bin spacing = fs / n_fft   (interpolation)
Δf ≈ fs / win_length  ·  Δt ≈ win_length / fs  →  Δf · Δt ≈ 1

In speech this shows up as the classic pair. A narrowband spectrogram uses a window covering several pitch periods — at a 118 Hz F0 the period is 8.5 ms, so a 25 ms window spans about three of them — and it resolves the individual harmonics of the voice as horizontal stripes. A wideband spectrogram uses a window shorter than one pitch period, say 5 ms, which smears the harmonics into a single broad formant band but resolves each glottal pulse as a vertical striation. Formant tracking wants wideband; pitch and harmonic structure want narrowband. Feature front ends for ASR and TTS sit deliberately in between, around 20–50 ms.

The window function itself matters. A rectangular window truncates the signal mid-cycle, and that discontinuity leaks energy across the whole spectrum, burying weak formants under the skirt of a strong one. Hann is the default because it tapers to zero at both ends and, at 50% or 75% overlap, satisfies the constant-overlap-add condition — the overlapped windows sum to a constant, so a resynthesis path can reconstruct without amplitude ripple. That property is what makes overlap-add work in Griffin-Lim and in streaming vocoders.

Hop length is the frame rate everything else inherits

Hop length is the parameter with the longest reach, because fs / hop_length is the frame rate of every tensor downstream: the duration predictor's units, the vocoder's upsampling factor, the alignment resolution of a forced aligner. Whisper's front end is 16 kHz, n_fft = 400 (25 ms), hop = 160 (10 ms), 80 mel bins — so 100 frames per second, 201 FFT bins per frame, and a 30-second window is exactly 480000 / 160 = 3000 frames. Tacotron 2 on LJSpeech is 22.05 kHz with n_fft = 1024 and hop = 256, which is 11.61 ms per frame and 86.13 frames per second; a decoder emitting 24 mel frames has produced 279 ms of audio. Get that number wrong and every duration in your dataset is scaled by a constant nobody notices until the speech is uniformly too fast.

Mel: 513 bins down to 80

A 1024-point FFT gives n_fft/2 + 1 = 513 non-redundant magnitude bins, evenly spaced 21.5 Hz apart at 22.05 kHz. That is the wrong basis for speech twice over: it is far more resolution than the ear has above 1 kHz, and it is correlated in a way that wastes model capacity. The mel scale is a monotonic warp, roughly linear below 1 kHz and logarithmic above it, that makes equal steps correspond to roughly equal perceived pitch distance.

HTK: m = 2595 · log₁₀(1 + f / 700)    f = 700 · (10m/2595 − 1)

There are two formulas in daily use and they disagree. The HTK form above is what librosa gives you with htk=True; the default, htk=False, is the Slaney form, linear at 200/3 Hz per mel below 1 kHz and logarithmic above. They produce different filter centre frequencies, so a mel computed one way is not the mel another model was trained on.

Building the bank is mechanical: pick n_mels, fmin and fmax; place n_mels + 2 points equally spaced in mel; map them back to Hz; and make filter i a triangle rising from point i to i+1 and falling to i+2. The result is a (n_mels, n_bins) matrix you multiply the power spectrum by. Two details bite. Slaney normalisation scales each triangle by 2/(hi − lo) so filters have equal area rather than equal peak, which changes the numeric range your model sees. And whenever the lowest triangles get narrower than the FFT bin spacing, those filters contain no bin at all and emit a hard log(1e-5) = −11.5 forever. The two ways to get there are a short window — a 5 ms window at 16 kHz gives n_fft = 128 and 125 Hz bins, far wider than the bottom triangles — and simply asking for too many bins: 128 mels over 0–4 kHz on an 8 kHz leg puts the first triangle's whole span below 21 Hz. At the usual 80 mels with a 25 ms window there are none, which is why it goes unnoticed until someone changes one of those two numbers. The visualization counts them live.

Finally the magnitudes are log-compressed, typically log(clamp(mel, min=1e-5)), because speech energy spans orders of magnitude and an L1 or L2 loss on linear power would be dominated entirely by the loudest vowels.

What it costs, and how it breaks

The latency is small but real. To emit the frame covering the first 25 ms you must have all 25 ms of it, so a streaming front end buffers one window — 25 ms, or 12.5 ms of lookahead relative to the frame's centre. Against an 800 ms conversational budget that is under 4%, and it is not where you optimise: endpointing hangover, at 200–700 ms, costs 8× to 28× as much — roughly an order of magnitude, and the actual place the budget goes. Worth knowing so you can say so rather than guess.

The characteristic failure is mel mismatch. An acoustic model and a vocoder trained on different front ends — HTK versus Slaney, fmax 8000 versus fs/2, natural log versus log₁₀, Slaney-normalised versus not — compose into a system where the vocoder receives mel it has never seen. It does not error; it produces a hoarse, buzzy, metallic voice that is recognisably the right words in the wrong throat. On a telephony leg there is a second version: a bank built for fmax = 8000 applied to 8 kHz audio upsampled to 16 kHz has a block of its top filters sitting over a band the network already deleted, and the model reads that silence as a feature. Be precise about how big that block is, because the mel scale is compressive and the intuition from linear frequency overshoots badly: 4–8 kHz is half the linear axis but only 24% of the mel axis, so of 80 triangles 18 sit entirely above 4 kHz and 2 more straddle it. That is a real waste of about a fifth of the representation, and a real band of constant log(1e-5) input the vocoder never saw in training — but it is a fifth, not a half.

PHI note. A mel spectrogram is not de-identified audio. 80 bins at 100 fps is 32 kB/s in float32 — bit for bit the same rate as 16-bit PCM at 16 kHz — it retains speaker identity well enough that x-vector systems are trained directly on it, and any vocoder inverts it back to intelligible speech. A "features only, we don't keep the audio" retention policy is not a de-identification control, and in a healthcare deployment the mel store inherits the same retention, access-log and BAA obligations as the recordings.

The arithmetic, written out

import numpy as np

# ─── mel scale (HTK). librosa's htk=True; htk=False is the Slaney variant. ───
def hz_to_mel(f): return 2595.0 * np.log10(1.0 + f / 700.0)
def mel_to_hz(m): return 700.0 * (10.0 ** (m / 2595.0) - 1.0)

def mel_filterbank(sr, n_fft, n_mels, fmin=0.0, fmax=None, norm="slaney"):
    fmax   = fmax or sr / 2
    n_bins = n_fft // 2 + 1
    fft_hz = np.linspace(0.0, sr / 2, n_bins)          # rfft bin centres

    # n_mels + 2 points equally spaced IN MEL, mapped back to Hz
    m_pts  = np.linspace(hz_to_mel(fmin), hz_to_mel(fmax), n_mels + 2)
    hz_pts = mel_to_hz(m_pts)

    fb = np.zeros((n_mels, n_bins))
    for i in range(n_mels):
        lo, mid, hi = hz_pts[i], hz_pts[i + 1], hz_pts[i + 2]
        rise = (fft_hz - lo) / (mid - lo)
        fall = (hi - fft_hz) / (hi - mid)
        fb[i] = np.maximum(0.0, np.minimum(rise, fall))
        if norm == "slaney":
            fb[i] *= 2.0 / (hi - lo)                   # equal area, not equal peak
    return fb

# ─── Whisper's front end, exactly ───────────────────────────────────────────
sr, n_fft, hop, n_mels = 16000, 400, 160, 80
fb = mel_filterbank(sr, n_fft, n_mels, fmin=0.0, fmax=8000.0)

print(fb.shape)                      # (80, 201)      201 = 400 // 2 + 1
print(sr / n_fft)                    # 40.0    Hz per FFT bin
print(sr / hop)                      # 100.0   frames per second
print((30 * sr) // hop)              # 3000    frames in a 30 s window
print((fb.sum(axis=1) == 0).sum())   # 0       empty filters — check this, it bites at 8 kHz

# ─── mel spectrogram + Tacotron-style dynamic range compression ─────────────
power  = np.abs(stft) ** 2           # (201, T)  |STFT|^2
mel    = fb @ power                  # (80,  T)  513->80 is a matmul, nothing more
logmel = np.log(np.clip(mel, 1e-5, None))

The line I'd actually say

"The STFT gives me a time-frequency picture, and the only real knob is the window: long window, sharp frequency, blurry time, and the reverse. Around 25 ms I resolve a 118 Hz talker's harmonics; at 5 ms I lose the harmonics and start seeing individual glottal pulses instead. Hop is the parameter I'd actually argue about, because fs over hop is the frame rate every downstream model inherits — 10 ms hop is 100 fps, and that's the unit my durations, my alignments and my vocoder's upsampling factor are all denominated in."

"The mel bank is then just a matmul: 80 triangles equally spaced in mel, so about 513 linear bins collapse to 80 perceptually-spaced ones. The thing I'd check first in a broken pipeline is that the acoustic model and the vocoder agree on exactly the same front end — HTK versus Slaney mel, the same fmax, the same log — because a mismatch doesn't throw, it just makes the voice buzz. And on an 8 kHz phone leg I'd check fmax against Nyquist and count the dead filters; a bank built for fmax = 8000 on a narrowband call has 18 of its 80 triangles sitting entirely over a band the network already threw away — not half, because mel is compressive and 4–8 kHz is only about a quarter of the mel axis."

Takeaways

  • Δf ≈ fs/win_length, Δt ≈ win_length/fs, and you cannot shrink both. Resolution is set by the window, not the DFT size — fs/n_fft is only bin spacing, and zero-padding shrinks it for free without resolving anything. A window spanning several pitch periods resolves harmonics (narrowband); one shorter than a pitch period resolves glottal pulses (wideband). Speech front ends sit at 20–50 ms deliberately.
  • Hop length is the frame rate everything inherits. 16 kHz / hop 160 = 100 fps and 3000 frames per 30 s Whisper window; 22.05 kHz / hop 256 = 86.13 fps and 11.61 ms per Tacotron 2 mel frame. Durations, alignments and vocoder upsampling are all denominated in it.
  • The mel bank is a (n_mels, n_bins) matmul. Points equally spaced in mel, mapped back to Hz, triangles between them — a few lines of numpy or PyTorch, and the same matrix must be baked identically into whatever runs at serve time, whether that is an ONNX graph or a Triton ensemble's preprocessing step.
  • Mel mismatch is the characteristic failure and it is silent. HTK vs Slaney, different fmax, natural log vs log₁₀, Slaney normalisation on or off — the vocoder gets out-of-domain mel and produces a hoarse, buzzy voice rather than an exception. Pin the front end in one place and assert on it in CI.
  • At 8 kHz, check fmax and count dead filters. Nyquist is 4 kHz, so a bank built for fmax = 8000 strands 18 of its 80 triangles entirely above it — about a fifth, not half, because 4–8 kHz is only 24% of the mel axis. Separately, narrow low-frequency triangles can contain zero FFT bins and emit a hard zero.
  • Mel is not de-identification. 80 bins × 100 fps × float32 is 32 kB/s — the same rate as the 16-bit PCM it came from — it carries speaker identity, and any vocoder inverts it to intelligible speech. In healthcare the feature store is PHI too.
