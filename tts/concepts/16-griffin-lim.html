16Griffin-Lim and Phase Reconstruction

A magnitude spectrogram is half a transform. Griffin-Lim guesses the other half by bouncing between the two constraints it has — provably never getting worse, provably never getting all the way there. The residue is the hollow, chorused, pre-echoing sound that made neural vocoders worth training.

Theory

Half the transform is missing

An STFT frame is complex. A spectrogram keeps |X| and throws away ∠X — less lossy than it sounds. With a Hann window of n_fft = 1024 and hop = 256, every 256 new samples buy you 513 one-sided magnitudes: about two real numbers per sample. Magnitude alone is still 2× overdetermined, which is why recovery is possible in principle (Nawab, Quatieri & Lim, 1983) — but not in closed form, because “these magnitudes all came from one real signal” couples every pair of overlapping frames.

Two sets. The consistency set is every array that really is the STFT of some time signal: a linear subspace, convex, projected onto by an inverse STFT and a forward STFT back-to-back. The magnitude set is every array whose magnitudes equal the target S: a product of circles, not convex. Griffin & Lim (1984) alternate the two — ISTFT by weighted overlap-add, STFT again, keep the phase that came back, put the target magnitude on top. They prove the distance never increases. They do not prove it reaches zero, and for a predicted — therefore inconsistent — spectrogram the intersection is usually empty, so the iteration settles on a critical point that depends on where it started.

What the residue sounds like

The artefact is not “wrong phase” being audible — the absolute phase of a steady tone is inaudible. It is incoherent overlap-add. At 75% overlap every output sample is a sum of four windowed frames, and when their phases disagree, harmonics that should add constructively partly cancel and re-add frame by frame. On voiced speech that is the hollow, slightly metallic, chorused voice everyone recognises from 2017-era demos. On a plosive it is pre-echo: burst energy spread over the whole window containing it, so you hear a whoosh before the /t/, lasting about one window — 46 ms for a 1024-point window at 22.05 kHz. On fricatives the inconsistency stays numerically high and almost nobody hears it, because phase relations inside noise carry little perceptual weight.

Mel makes it worse, and iterations will not save you

Griffin-Lim inverts linear magnitude. From an 80-bin mel you must first invert the filterbank, and 80 → 513 is underdetermined: the pseudo-inverse or non-negative least-squares fit (librosa's mel_to_stft) smears each band across its triangle first. Tacotron 1 sidestepped that by predicting linear-scale magnitude with a CBHG post-net, then running 50 iterations on magnitudes raised to the power 1.2 — a sharpening trick the paper credits with reducing artefacts. Hand it a mel and the ceiling is set by the filterbank inverse, not by the iteration count.

Cost, and where it lands in a turn

One iteration is one ISTFT plus one STFT: at 22.05 kHz with hop 256, 32 iterations is about 5,500 length-1024 FFTs per second of audio, roughly 0.28 GFLOP/s. The arithmetic is free; the shape is the problem. Every iteration touches the whole chunk, so the chunk is pure lookahead — nothing ships until all of it exists and every pass has run, where a streaming GAN vocoder emits after a handful of frames. Against the ~150 ms of time-to-first-audio TTS gets in an 800 ms turn, whole-utterance Griffin-Lim is out, and chunked Griffin-Lim buys that back in seams: each chunk converges to its own phase and the boundary does not match.

Fast Griffin-Lim (Perraudin, Balazs & Søndergaard, 2013) extrapolates the consistency projection, c ← d + α(d − dprev), with α = 0.99 the default in both librosa.griffinlim and torchaudio.transforms.GriffinLim. It reaches a given error in far fewer rounds, and gives up the monotonicity guarantee doing it, so the curve can tick upward.

Keep it anyway. No weights, no training data, no GPU, deterministic: the fallback when a vocoder checkpoint will not load, and the diagnostic that separates blame — run it on predicted mel and on ground-truth mel, and if both sound equally bad the acoustic model is not your problem. In healthcare telephony, nothing here was trained on caller voice, so the fallback adds no PHI-derived weights. The phone leg hides part of the damage, since the 3.4 kHz cutoff removes the top-band sizzle — but pre-echo and chorusing sit inside the narrowband.

Visualization — the error falls, then stops falling

# ---- what a magnitude spectrogram left you ---------------------------------
n_fft, hop, sr = 1024, 256, 22050
bins             = n_fft // 2 + 1     # 513 one-sided magnitudes per frame
reals_per_sample = bins / hop         # 2.00 -> magnitude alone is 2x overdetermined
frames_per_s     = sr / hop           # 86.1 frames per second of audio

# ---- Griffin-Lim: alternate the two projections ----------------------------
def griffin_lim(S, n_iter=32, momentum=0.99):     # S = target |STFT|, non-negative
    c, prev = S * exp(2j*pi*rng.random(S.shape)), 0   # librosa's init='random'
    for k in range(n_iter):
        x  = istft(c)                 # weighted overlap-add
        d  = stft(x)                  # P_consistent: the STFT x actually has
        sc = norm(abs(d) - S) / norm(S)   # spectral convergence; monotone if momentum=0
        e, prev = d + momentum * (d - prev), d        # fast GL (Perraudin 2013)
        c  = S * e / (abs(e) + 1e-16) # P_magnitude: keep phase, restore |X|
    return istft(c)

The line I'd actually say

"A spectrogram is a complex STFT with the phase deleted, and at 75% overlap that still leaves about two real numbers per sample — recoverable in principle, just not in closed form. Griffin-Lim alternates two projections: inverse STFT then forward STFT, landing on something that is some real signal's transform, then put the target magnitude back and keep the phase that came out. The 1984 proof is that the error never increases; it says nothing about reaching zero, and against a predicted spectrogram there's usually no exact solution to reach."

"What survives is incoherent overlap-add — four frames summing at each sample with phases that don't agree — so you get the chorused, hollow voice, and pre-echo smeared one window ahead of every plosive. Phase isn't audible; the frames failing to add up is. And it isn't FLOPs that rule it out as a vocoder — thirty-odd iterations is under a GFLOP per second of audio — it's that every iteration needs the whole chunk, so the chunk becomes lookahead. I'd still keep it wired in: no weights and no GPU, so it's the fallback if the vocoder won't load, and the fastest way to tell whether a bad sample is the acoustic model's fault or the vocoder's."

Takeaways

  • Magnitude is 2× overdetermined, not underdetermined. bins/hop = (n_fft/2+1)/(n_fft/4) ≈ 2 real numbers per sample at 75% overlap , so recovery is possible in principle (Nawab, Quatieri & Lim, 1983) — just not in closed form, because overlapping frames constrain each other.
  • Alternating projection: convex set, non-convex set. ISTFT→STFT projects onto the consistency subspace; re-imposing S projects onto a product of circles. Griffin & Lim (1984) prove non-increase, not convergence to zero, and against a predicted spectrogram the intersection is usually empty — so it lands on an initialisation-dependent point.
  • The artefact is incoherent overlap-add, not audible phase. Chorused, hollow voiced speech; pre-echo about one window ahead of every plosive; fricatives numerically bad and perceptually fine. Never score it with waveform SNR — a globally phase-shifted reconstruction sounds identical and scores terribly. Use spectral convergence plus ASR WER on the output.
  • Mel bounds you before the iterations do. 80→513 via pseudo-inverse or NNLS smears each band first; Tacotron 1 predicted linear magnitude with a CBHG post-net and ran 50 iterations on magnitudes raised to the power 1.2. Iterations cannot recover what the filterbank inverse already blurred.
  • Momentum buys rounds, and costs the guarantee. Fast Griffin-Lim's d + α(d − d_prev), α = 0.99 by default in librosa.griffinlim and PyTorch's torchaudio.transforms.GriffinLim, converges much faster but can tick upward — log the error curve rather than trust a fixed iteration count.
  • Wire it in as the fallback, not the vocoder. No weights, no GPU, no PHI-derived training data — the CPU path when a Triton-served HiFi-GAN checkpoint fails to load, alarmed on fallback rate in Prometheus. But every iteration needs the whole chunk, so the chunk is lookahead: 300 ms of it against ~150 ms of TTFA in an 800 ms turn.
