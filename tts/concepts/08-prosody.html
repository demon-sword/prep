08Prosody: Pitch, Duration and Energy

The three signals that separate speech from a talking clock — F0 carrying intonation and question versus statement, per-phoneme duration carrying rhythm and emphasis, energy carrying stress — and why predicting them explicitly gives control an end-to-end model buries.

Theory

Three numbers on one grid

Prosody is what is left of an utterance once you take the words out — melody, rhythm, loudness — as three signals on the mel spectrogram's frame grid. Every TTS system predicts all three: on tensors you can reach, or implicitly where you cannot.

F0 is the rate at which the vocal folds open and close; adult ranges run roughly 85–180 Hz for men and 165–255 Hz for women. Pitch is perceived roughly logarithmically, so models work in log-F0 or semitones around a per-speaker mean rather than in Hz. F0 exists only in voiced frames: during /s/ or a stop closure it is undefined, not zero, and conflating the two teaches the predictor to aim at a value that never occurs. Duration is how many frames each phone occupies — at 22.05 kHz with a 256-sample hop a frame is 1000·256/22050 = 11.61 ms, so a 120 ms vowel is about ten frames and the duration predictor fixes utterance length before a sample exists. Energy is the per-frame L2 norm of the magnitude spectrum, the correlate of stress.

What each one carries

F0 carries sentence type: an English statement falls to the bottom of the speaker's range — L-L% in ToBI notation — and a yes/no question ends on a rise, H-H%. It also marks focus: a pitch accent on one word plus range compression after it is how "can you come in on Tuesday" differs from "can you come in on Tuesday" with identical phonemes. Duration carries rhythm and, at boundaries, final lengthening — English stretches the last syllable before a phrase break, which with the terminal fall is how a listener knows your turn ended.

Predicting them explicitly

FastSpeech 2's variance adaptor is the canonical arrangement: a duration predictor emits a log-domain length per phone, a length regulator repeats each encoder state that many times onto the frame grid, and pitch and energy predictors add quantised embeddings to the expanded sequence. Targets come from forced alignment for durations and a pitch tracker — RAPT, YIN/pYIN, or WORLD's DIO with StoneMask — for F0. Hence the control surface: 1/α on durations is speaking rate, a scale on the semitone offsets is expressiveness, an override on one phone is emphasis. Tacotron 2 and VITS bury the same prosody in an attention matrix or a latent, where there is no knob.

The failure mode: collapse to a central reading

Text-to-prosody is one-to-many — the same sentence has a rise reading and a fall reading — and a deterministic regression head returns a single central statistic of that distribution rather than a sample from it: an L2 loss converges to the conditional mean, an L1 loss to the conditional median. Neither is a valid reading when the target is bimodal, and on a rise/fall mixture both land in the middle, so the contour flattens: right phones, right words, delivery like a speaking clock. That is the characteristic FastSpeech-family artifact, and why VITS uses a stochastic duration predictor.

On a phone call the cost is not charm. Flat prosody breaks turn-taking: with no terminal fall and no final lengthening the caller cannot tell you have finished, so they wait through dead air or talk over you. Intonation does survive the line, though — the passband is about 300–3400 Hz, so a 110 Hz fundamental is filtered out entirely, but its harmonics at 330, 440, 550 Hz and up are spaced by exactly F0 and the ear recovers the missing fundamental. Telephony costs fricatives, not melody. And an F0 track is regulated data: pitch statistics are speaker-characterising, so give a contour the retention and access path of the recorded call itself. 

# Variance adaptor, one utterance. Frame grid: 22.05 kHz, hop 256.
SR, HOP  = 22050, 256
FRAME_MS = 1000.0 * HOP / SR                # 11.61 ms per frame

# 1. duration: predicted as log(d+1), rounded onto the frame grid
d = np.maximum(1, np.round(np.exp(log_d_pred) - 1)).astype(int)
d = np.maximum(1, np.round(d / alpha))      # alpha=1.15 -> 1.15x speaking rate

# 2. length regulator: phoneme states -> frame states
frames   = np.repeat(enc_out, d, axis=0)    # (sum(d), d_model)
n_frames = int(d.sum())                     # audio_ms = n_frames * 11.61

# 3. pitch: log-F0, unvoiced interpolated, z-scored per speaker, binned
lf0 = interp_unvoiced(np.where(voiced, np.log(f0), np.nan))
z   = (lf0 - spk_mean) / spk_std            # speaker-free, semitone-like
p_b = np.clip(np.digitize(z * rho, PITCH_BINS), 0, 255)   # rho = expressiveness
e_b = np.clip(np.digitize(energy,  ENERGY_BINS), 0, 255)

# 4. additive conditioning -> any of the three is overridable at serve time
h = frames + pitch_emb[p_b] + energy_emb[e_b]

The line I'd actually say

"Prosody is three tensors on the mel frame grid — log-F0, per-phone duration, per-frame energy — and I want them predicted explicitly, the FastSpeech 2 variance-adaptor way, because that gives me a speaking-rate knob, an expressiveness knob and per-word emphasis at serve time. VITS or Tacotron generate the same prosody inside a latent or an attention matrix, where I can't reach it."

"The failure mode is collapse to a central reading: text-to-prosody is one-to-many, and a deterministic regression head gives you one statistic of that distribution — the conditional mean under L2, the median under L1 — so a rise/fall target lands in the middle and you ship a speaking clock. On a phone line that's a turn-taking bug: callers key off the terminal fall and final lengthening to know you've finished, so a flat contour gets you barged in on mid-sentence. And intonation actually survives narrowband — a 110 Hz fundamental is below the 300 Hz passband edge, but the harmonics are spaced by F0 and the ear reconstructs the missing fundamental. The fix is stochastic duration prediction, or a flow or diffusion prosody head, so you sample a reading instead of averaging them."

Takeaways

  • Three signals, one frame grid. Log-F0 (voiced frames only — undefined, not zero), duration in frames, energy per frame. At 22.05 kHz with hop 256 that grid is 11.61 ms, and the duration predictor fixes utterance length before a sample exists.
  • Explicit prediction is a control surface. The variance adaptor conditions additively, so 1/α on durations is speaking rate and a scale on the semitone offsets is expressiveness. Build it in PyTorch, export the serving path to ONNX: a few conv layers, well under a millisecond of a time-to-first-audio budget in the low hundreds.
  • The characteristic failure is flatness, and it is a loss-function problem. On a one-to-many target L2 returns the conditional mean and L1 the conditional median — both one central value where the truth is bimodal, so the rise/fall distinction dies. VITS answers it with a stochastic duration predictor. Track F0 span per utterance as a Prometheus gauge; flatness is silent otherwise.
  • Flat prosody is a turn-taking bug. Terminal fall plus final lengthening is how a caller knows you have stopped. Lose it and you get barge-in mid-sentence or dead air — where this page hands off to endpointing policy.
  • Narrowband keeps the melody. A 110 Hz F0 sits below the 300–3400 Hz passband, but its harmonics are spaced by F0 and the missing fundamental is perceived anyway. Telephony costs fricatives, not intonation — do not widen pitch range for a problem the phone line did not cause.
  • An F0 track is speaker-characterising. Pitch statistics plus a transcript are a re-identification handle, so give contours the retention and access path of the recorded audio, not the metrics store.
