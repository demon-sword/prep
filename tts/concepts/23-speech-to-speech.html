23Full-Duplex Speech-to-Speech Models

The cascade turns a phone call into text and back again, and every millisecond and every non-word cue is spent at those two boundaries. A speech-to-speech model keeps the audio — and gives up the text checkpoint where all of your control, biasing and compliance currently live. This is a live trade, not a migration.

Theory

Three different things get called speech-to-speech

A turn-based audio API that swallows an audio blob and returns an audio blob is a cascade with the seams hidden — you have outsourced the stages, not removed them. A streaming audio-in/audio-out model still gated by an external VAD is half duplex: it listens, then it talks, and the endpointer is still yours. Full duplex means the model consumes the caller's stream and emits its own on every frame, so silence, overlap and backchannels are all in-distribution. Kyutai's Moshi is the clearest published instance: two parallel audio streams — the model's and the user's — at a 12.5 Hz frame rate, with a text stream (“Inner Monologue”) predicted ahead of the acoustic tokens. Even the flagship full-duplex model keeps text in the loop as a prior; the dichotomy is softer than the marketing.

Where the milliseconds actually go

# full-duplex S2S: four stages collapse into commit-then-generate
net_in                            60

net_out                           60

                                 725

The saving is not that the model is faster per token; it is that the cascade pays four sequential commit points and the S2S model pays one. Human turn transitions cluster around a couple of hundred milliseconds, and past roughly 800 ms a caller treats the pause as a broken line. The cascade above is 1190 and the S2S 725 — and 500 of the cascade's number is one threshold. So the honest cascade counter-move is not a faster GPU but speculative prefill on ASR partials, overlapping the LLM with the endpoint wait at the cost of discarding that work whenever the caller keeps talking.

What the text boundary throws away

# information surviving each representation, per second (approximate)
mu-law 8 kHz        8000 x 8 bits            = 64,000 bps
codec tokens        12.5 Hz x 8 cb x 11 bits =  1,100 bps
ASR text @ 150 wpm  13.75 chars x 8 bits     =    110 bps

Roughly 580:1 from the wire to the words. What is in the discarded 99.8% is not decoration: F0 slope, the hesitation before a number, whether “no” is refusal or surprise, a sharp inhale that means the caller is about to interrupt, and the fact that they started speaking 40 ms before the agent finished. Turn-taking runs on exactly those cues, which is why a cascade endpointer is guessing from silence duration while an S2S model has heard the falling pitch. The TTS side then has to re-invent prosody from words alone, so the cascade tends to sound fine sentence by sentence and feel wrong across a turn.

What the cascade keeps that you cannot get back

Text is where the control surface is. Contextual biasing pins drug names and member IDs that no acoustic model gets right unprompted. Redaction runs before anything is written to storage. Tool-call arguments are validated before they hit a scheduling API. A policy guardrail inspects the answer before the caller hears it. Per-stage timers attribute a latency regression to the stage that caused it. And any component is swappable without retraining the others. With an audio-native model none of those hooks exist unless the vendor exposes an intermediate text stream — and if it does not, your only guardrail is a shadow ASR reading the output after it has already been played.

The transcript is the other quiet requirement. In healthcare it is the record, and you will run an ASR for it regardless. So S2S does not remove ASR from your bill; it removes it from the critical path and leaves the cost, plus a second model to keep aligned with the one actually talking.

Failure modes, and which architecture lets you find them

The cascade fails in ways with a line number: the endpointer fires at 700 ms inside a dictated ID, the ASR returns the wrong drug, the vocoder buzzes. Each has a stage, a metric and a knob. The S2S failures are learned behaviours: it yields the floor to a cough, or it will not yield at all; it drifts speaker identity mid-turn; it produces a confident answer adjacent to the question with no text checkpoint anywhere before the audio. The fix is data or a prompt, and the loop is days, not the minutes it takes to change a threshold. That is the real trade — not quality, but mean time to diagnosis.

The phone line, and PHI

Most S2S models are trained at 24 kHz on relatively clean speech. A PSTN leg hands them 8 kHz mu-law with everything above 3.4 kHz gone, plus jitter and packet loss — a band and a noise profile they barely saw in training, and no front end fixes it because the front end is the model. Evaluate on real narrowband call audio. And the caller's voice now sits in the model's context instead of being converted to text and discarded: voice is a biometric identifier, so retention, region and BAA scope bind on the audio context itself. The cascade at least gives you a point where you can redact before persisting; with S2S nothing is redactable before it is in context.

How I would actually ship it

Hybrid, per capability rather than per product. Full duplex is genuinely better at the conversational shell — turn-taking, backchannels, graceful interruption — and worse at anything transactional, where a wrong token is a wrong appointment. So: S2S for triage and small talk with a strict handoff, cascade for identity verification, scheduling and anything that writes. Or keep the cascade and steal only the good part, letting an acoustic turn-taking model drive the endpointer while text still carries the content. Anyone who tells you one of these has already won is selling it.

The line I'd actually say

“The cascade's latency problem isn't that any one stage is slow — it's that there are four sequential commit points. Endpoint wait, ASR finalisation, first token, first audio, plus two network legs is around 1.2 seconds, and half a second of that is one silence threshold. A full-duplex model collapses those four into one learned decision to start speaking, so you're looking at roughly 700 ms on the same call, and it's making that decision from the pitch contour and the hesitation rather than from a silence timer.”

“What you pay for it is the text checkpoint. That boundary throws away about 99.8% of the bits — 64 kbps of mu-law down to roughly a hundred bits per second of words — but it's also where contextual biasing for drug names lives, where redaction happens before storage, where the guardrail runs before the caller hears anything, and where the transcript that is legally the record comes from. Give that up and your only guardrail reads the output after it's been played.”

“So I'd deploy it per capability, not per product: full duplex for the conversational shell, cascade for anything transactional. And I'd flag two things people skip — these models are trained on 24 kHz clean speech and a phone leg hands them 8 kHz mu-law, so evaluate on real call audio; and the caller's voice is now sitting in the model context, which is a biometric identifier, so retention and BAA scope bind on the audio itself.”

Takeaways

  • Count commit points, not stages. 60 + 500 + 120 + 320 + 130 + 60 ≈ 1190 ms of cascade against ~725 for a commit-then-generate model. S2S wins because four sequential decisions become one, not because its tokens are faster.
  • “Speech-to-speech” names three different systems. A blob-in/blob-out API is a hidden cascade; a VAD-gated streaming model is half duplex; full duplex models both streams every frame. Moshi keeps a text stream as a prior even so — the dichotomy is softer than it sounds.
  • The ASR boundary is a ~580:1 information cut. 64 kbps mu-law → ~1.1 kbps codec tokens → ~110 bps of text, and the discarded part is precisely the turn-taking evidence. It is also where biasing, redaction and guardrails attach.
  • The real trade is mean time to diagnosis. Cascade failures have a stage and a knob; S2S failures are learned behaviour fixed with data over days. Keep per-stage timers in Prometheus with p95 panels in Grafana, and a shadow ASR writing transcripts to Kafka — you need that transcript for the record whichever architecture you ship.
  • Evaluate on the phone leg, not the demo. An audio-native model trained at 24 kHz meets 8 kHz mu-law with jitter, and you cannot patch it with a front end because the front end is the model. Serving it behind Triton does not change what it was trained on.
  • Audio-native context is PHI you cannot redact first. Voice is a biometric identifier and it now lives in the model's context, so retention, region and BAA scope bind on the audio — the cascade at least offers a text point to redact before persisting.
