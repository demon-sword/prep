11Attention Failure Modes in TTS

Skipped words, repeated syllables and babble that never terminates are three readings of one picture: a non-monotonic attention matrix. Location-sensitive attention, guided-attention training, inference windowing and hard monotonic alignment each bound the damage — and none of them make a confused model align.

Theory

The matrix is the diagnosis

An autoregressive TTS decoder emits one mel frame per step, and at each step produces a distribution a_t over the L encoder symbols. Stack the rows: a T×L alignment matrix. Healthy synthesis draws a narrow, monotonically rising ridge: each frame attends to one or two phonemes and the position never goes back. The slope is the speaking rate — at a 12.5 ms hop and roughly 7 frames per phoneme, a phoneme occupies about 87 ms, so 17 phonemes take about 120 frames, 1.5 s. Every characteristic failure of this architecture is a defect in that ridge, and you can classify it before listening to a sample.

Four defects, four sounds

Skipping is a forward jump: the ridge leaps several symbols in one frame, leaving a column band with almost no mass, and those phonemes are never rendered. It sounds like a clean utterance with a word missing — in "do not take two tablets", the difference between a correct instruction and a dangerous one. Repeating is a backward jump: the position regresses, re-traverses phonemes it already emitted, and the caller hears "ta-ta-take" or a word said twice. Collapse is loss of sharpness: rows flatten toward uniform, the context vector becomes an average over the whole encoder, and the mel degenerates into a smeared drone — not silence, since a vocoder like HiFi-GAN renders out-of-domain mel faithfully as a buzzing hum. The position never reaches the end of the text, so the stop token never fires and generation runs to max_decoder_steps: at the usual default of 1000, 12.5 s of noise. The fourth, early stopping, is the mirror image — the stop scalar crosses threshold mid-sentence and the utterance truncates mid-word.

Why it happens at all

Content-based attention scores every encoder state independently and softmaxes over all of them. Nothing in that graph forbids a backward jump; monotonicity is a habit learned from data, not a constraint. Location-sensitive attention (Chorowski et al., 2015) adds a memory of where the model has been — the cumulative alignment, convolved into the energy — which discourages regressions without ruling them out. The habit breaks off-distribution: utterances longer than any seen in training, repeated words that look identical to a content scorer, long digit strings, text normalisation left unexpanded. Free-running inference compounds it — the decoder conditions on its own outputs, so one bad frame biases the next query.

Measuring it without listening

Two numbers do most of the work. Focus rate, from the FastSpeech paper, is F = (1/T) Σ_t max_s a_ts: the mean peak height per row, near 1 when sharp, falling toward 1/L when collapsed. Coverage asks the columns — max_t a_ts per symbol, anything below a threshold was never spoken — and backward moves of argmax_s a_ts catch repeats. In training, the guided-attention loss (DC-TTS, Tachibana et al., 2018) makes the same idea a gradient: weight W_ts = 1 − exp(−(t/T − s/L)² / 2g²), g = 0.2, and penalise mean(A ⊙ W).

The ladder of fixes, and what each really buys

Ordered by freedom removed: guided attention shapes training only; inference windowing masks the softmax to [p, p+w], making a backward jump unrepresentable; forward attention (Zhang et al., 2018) sums only over monotonic paths, with an optional transition agent controlling advance speed; location-relative mechanisms — GMM attention and Dynamic Convolution Attention (Battenberg et al., 2020) — drop content-based scoring and add a fixed prior filter biasing the position forward every step, which is what stabilises long-form synthesis; MoChA makes the decision discrete; and Monotonic Alignment Search in Glow-TTS and VITS finds the best monotonic path rather than attending at all. The end of the ladder is FastSpeech 2, where a duration predictor and length regulator make alignment a length expansion — the failure deleted by construction, paid for in prosodic variety.

Be honest about the middle of that ladder: a monotonic window does not fix alignment, it converts one failure into a cheaper one. Forced monotonic, a stutter becomes an over-long phone, skipped phonemes get squeezed in rushed rather than dropped, and a collapse becomes a mumble that terminates instead of 12.5 s of buzz. Terminating and wrong beats unbounded and wrong on a call — but it is still wrong.

On a live call, with PHI

Detection has to be online, because streaming has already sent audio to the caller — and focus rate plus argmax cost one max over L floats per frame, nothing against a 12.5 ms frame. The three guards worth running are in the code below. On abort, cancel synthesis the way barge-in does, flush the outbound buffer and play a pre-rendered prompt; the alternative is dead air inside an 800 ms turn budget, or a drone the caller hears as a dead line. In healthcare a dropped not or a dropped digit in a dose readback is a clinical error, not an artifact, so log the diagonality score, frame count and template ID — never the synthesised text, which is PHI-derived and inherits its retention rules.

# 1. TRAINING: guided attention loss (DC-TTS, Tachibana 2018), g = 0.2.
#    A: (T, L) attention.  Pays the model to stay near the diagonal.
W     = 1 - exp(-((t/T - s/L)**2) / (2 * g**2))    # (T, L), 0 on the diagonal
L_att = (A * W).mean()

# 2. INFERENCE: monotonic window + prior forward bias (the DCA idea).
#    A backward jump stops being representable, not just unlikely.
mask = (idx >= p) & (idx <= p + W_HALF)            # idx: (L,) encoder positions
a    = softmax(e + log(mask))                      # renormalise inside the window
p    = max(p + PRIOR_ADVANCE, argmax(a))           # never stalls, never regresses

# 3. ONLINE GUARD: one max over L floats per frame — free at a 12.5 ms hop.
focus     = a.max()                                # FastSpeech focus rate, per row
focus_ema = 0.9 * focus_ema + 0.1 * focus
backward += (argmax(a) < prev_pos)
prev_pos  = argmax(a)

# ~7 frames/phoneme at a 12.5 ms hop -> ~87 ms each; 17 phonemes ~ 120 frames.
budget = 1.6 * 7 * n_phonemes
if t > budget:        abort("overrun")     # stop token never fired
if focus_ema < 0.30:  abort("collapse")    # drone, not silence
if backward > 3:      abort("stutter")
# abort -> cancel synthesis, flush the outbound buffer, play a cached prompt.

The line I'd actually say

"All three classic failures are the same picture. Plot the attention matrix, decoder frames against encoder symbols: healthy synthesis is a narrow monotone ridge. A forward jump is a skipped word, a backward jump is a stutter, and rows flattening toward uniform are collapse — the context vector becomes an average over the whole encoder, so the mel is a smear and the vocoder renders it as a buzz, not silence. And since the position never reaches the end of the text, the stop token never fires: you run to max_decoder_steps, and 1000 steps at a 12.5 ms hop is 12.5 seconds of that down the line."

"I'd catch it online rather than offline: focus rate, which is just the mean peak of each attention row, plus argmax regressions. That's one max over the encoder length per frame, free against a 12.5 ms frame. Guards on overrun, on focus dropping below about 0.3, and on repeated backward jumps; on trip, cancel synthesis, flush the outbound buffer and play a cached prompt, because dead air inside an 800 ms turn is bad but a drone is worse. The fixes ladder from guided attention loss in training, through inference windowing and forward attention, to location-relative attention like DCA with its forward prior, to monotonic alignment search in VITS. Worth being honest, though: a monotonic window doesn't make a confused model align, it converts twelve seconds of babble into a mumble that terminates. If I actually need the failure gone by construction, that's the argument for an explicit duration predictor — FastSpeech 2 — and I pay for it in prosodic variety."

Takeaways

  • One picture, four diagnoses. Forward jump → skipped word; backward jump → repeated syllable; flattened rows → collapse and a stop token that never fires; premature stop scalar → truncation mid-word. Dump the T×L matrix from your PyTorch inference hook before you dump audio.
  • Score it, don't listen to it. Focus rate F = (1/T)Σ_t max_s a_ts (FastSpeech), per-symbol coverage max_t a_ts, and argmax regressions catch all three offline; the guided-attention weight 1−exp(−(t/T−s/L)²/2g²), g=0.2, is the same idea as a training loss.
  • Collapse is unbounded, which is why it is the production risk. 1000 max_decoder_steps at a 12.5 ms hop is 12.5 s of buzz and ~10× the GPU-seconds the utterance was budgeted. Cap the steps, and export focus rate and steps-per-phoneme to Prometheus so the alert fires on the distribution, not on one angry call.
  • The fix ladder removes freedom, in order. Location-sensitive attention discourages regressions; guided attention loss shapes training; inference windowing and forward attention make them unrepresentable; DCA's prior filter guarantees forward movement for long-form; monotonic alignment search in Glow-TTS/VITS drops soft attention entirely.
  • Monotonicity bounds damage, it does not fix alignment. Forced monotonic, a stutter becomes an over-long phone, a skip becomes rushed phonemes, and a collapse becomes a terminating mumble. Deleting the failure by construction means explicit durations — FastSpeech 2 — at the cost of prosodic variety.
  • On a call it is a safety issue, not an artifact. A dropped "not" or a dropped digit in a dose readback is a clinical error; abort like a barge-in, flush the outbound buffer, play a cached prompt, and log the diagonality score and template ID rather than the PHI-derived text.
