13Autoregressive vs Non-Autoregressive TTS

One dependency edge separates the two families; time-to-first-audio, tail latency, batching and prosodic variety are all downstream of it. The question is not which is better, but which number you are optimising and which percentile you are willing to lose.

Theory

The only difference that matters

Both families map the same phonemes onto the same 80-bin mel and hand it to the same vocoder; one dependency edge separates them. An autoregressive decoder computes mel frame t from the frames it already produced, so T frames cost T serial forward passes and T itself is unknown until a stop token fires. A non-autoregressive decoder predicts a duration per phoneme, expands to a T known before decoding begins, and computes every frame in one pass. A reduction factor r > 1 emits r frames per autoregressive step and divides the step count — Tacotron 1's cheapest lever, dropped in Tacotron 2.

Do the arithmetic

At 22.05 kHz with a 256-sample hop a frame is 11.61 ms of audio, so a second of speech is 86 frames. Take an illustrative 1.5 ms per autoregressive decoder step; the constant is yours to measure, the structure is not. A five-second reply is 431 frames, so mel generation costs 431 × 1.5 = 647 ms against roughly 26 ms for one non-autoregressive pass over the same 431 frames. Vocoding is common to both and nearly free: HiFi-GAN V1 is reported at 3.7 MHz on a V100, about 168× real time, so those five seconds cost about 30 ms either way.

The number the caller actually hears

Total synthesis time is a rout, and the wrong metric. What the caller experiences is time-to-first-audio. An autoregressive decoder can hand its first 30 frames to the vocoder while it is still decoding frame 200: 30 × 1.5 + 30 × 0.07 ≈ 47 ms, flat in utterance length. Classic non-autoregressive decoding cannot: its decoder self-attention is non-causal over the whole frame sequence, so no frame is final until the pass ends and first-audio tracks T. On a short turn the single pass wins both numbers; on a long read-out the flat autoregressive curve crosses under it, unless you chunk at clause boundaries and pay in seams.

Where an 800 ms turn goes

Endpoint hangover ~250 ms, ASR finalisation ~80 ms, LLM time-to-first-token ~280 ms, egress plus jitter buffer ~90 ms: about 700 ms goes before the synthesiser emits a sample, leaving roughly 100 ms for time-to-first-audio — and the two families reach it differently. Autoregressive clears it only by streaming: waiting out that five-second reply is 676 ms of TTS alone, nearly seven budgets, against 47 ms when the first chunk vocodes early. One non-autoregressive pass clears it un-chunked at this length — under 30 ms to first audio — but that figure climbs with T where the autoregressive one is flat, reaching 93 ms at twelve seconds. Chunking is optional for one family, mandatory for the other.

The tail is the real argument

Autoregressive latency is unbounded by construction: training is teacher-forced, inference is not, errors compound, and the stop token is one more prediction. When it never fires the decoder runs to max_decoder_steps — 1000 in NVIDIA's Tacotron 2 configuration — burning 1.5 s of GPU to emit 11.6 s of babble the caller may hear. Be precise about the percentile: a 0.5% failure rate corrupts the worst 0.5% of turns, so it shows at p99.5 and above and leaves p99 clean — you need about 1% before p99 moves. A destroyed turn is a call the caller ends, and batching magnifies it: an autoregressive batch keeps stepping until its longest member terminates, so one runaway stalls every call sharing it. Non-autoregressive batches are uniform — identical known work per item — so one side's capacity planning is arithmetic and the other's is queueing theory.

Quality — get the direction right

The same text supports many valid prosodies. An autoregressive decoder conditions each frame on those it just emitted, so it commits to one reading rather than averaging them. A duration or pitch predictor trained with MSE returns the conditional mean instead, which is why plain non-autoregressive output sounds tidy and flat. Do not overstate it: stochastic duration prediction, flow and diffusion decoders and adversarial losses have closed much of that gap. Non-autoregressive is faster and far more predictable, not more natural, and the naturalness gap is narrowing while the tail-latency gap is structural.

On a call, and on PHI

The largest lever open to either family is caching audio for phrases repeated on every call, and that is where the compliance edge sits: cache the fixed greeting, never the clause carrying an appointment time and a member name, and key on template ID rather than rendered text, so caller details never become a lookup key that outlives the call.

The line I'd actually say

"They differ by one dependency edge. Autoregressive decoding conditions frame t on the frames before it, so you pay one forward pass per frame and you don't know how many frames there are until the stop token fires. Non-autoregressive predicts durations, expands to a known frame count, and does the whole mel in one pass. At an 11.6 ms hop, a five-second reply is 431 frames — roughly 650 ms of serial decode against tens of milliseconds for one pass."

"But total time isn't what the caller hears. Autoregressive can stream its first chunk to the vocoder while it's still decoding, so its time-to-first-audio is flat in utterance length — about 50 ms — whereas a non-causal parallel decoder has to finish before anything is final, so its first-audio time grows with T unless you chunk at clause boundaries. What actually decides it for me on telephony is the tail: the stop token is a prediction, and when it fails you run to max_decoder_steps and hand the caller ten seconds of babble, and in a batch that one runaway stalls every call sharing it. So I'd say non-autoregressive is faster and far more predictable, not more natural — the naturalness gap has largely closed with stochastic duration and flow decoders, while the tail-latency gap is structural."

Takeaways

  • One edge, everything else downstream. Autoregressive: T serial passes, T unknown until a stop token. Non-autoregressive: T from a duration predictor, one pass. Latency, batching and prosody differences all reduce to that.
  • Optimise time-to-first-audio, not total time. Streaming autoregressive first-audio is flat in utterance length (~50 ms at 1.5 ms/step with a 30-frame chunk); non-autoregressive first-audio grows with T because decoder attention is non-causal — chunk at clause boundaries to flatten it, and accept the seams.
  • Non-autoregressive is faster and more predictable, not more natural. MSE on durations and pitch returns the conditional mean, so prosody flattens; stochastic duration and flow/diffusion decoders have narrowed that, while the tail gap has not narrowed at all.
  • Name the percentile. A 0.5% stop-token failure corrupts p99.5 and above, not p99 — but it burns max_decoder_steps × ms_step of GPU and, under dynamic batching, blocks every call in the batch. Export step counts and stop-token probability from PyTorch to Prometheus and alert on the ratio, not the mean.
  • Uniform work is an ops property. Fixed T makes batches identical, which is what makes a Triton or ONNX deployment size-able by arithmetic; autoregressive batching is queueing theory with head-of-line blocking.
  • Both families cache, and that is the PHI edge. Reuse audio for fixed phrases, key on template ID rather than rendered text, and never cache the clause carrying a name or an appointment time.
