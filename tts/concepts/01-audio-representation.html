01Digital Audio Representation

Sample rate, bit depth and PCM as the substrate everything else sits on — why 16 kHz is the ASR convention and 24 kHz the TTS one, what Nyquist actually forbids, and how a naive resample between them folds energy back into the speech band as a metallic buzz the vocoder faithfully reproduces.

Theory

PCM is two numbers and a convention

Linear pulse-code modulation is a list of amplitude measurements taken at a fixed interval. Everything downstream — the STFT, the mel filterbank, the vocoder output, the RTP payload on the wire — is a transformation of that list. Two numbers define it. The sample rate fs is how many measurements per second; the bit depth is how finely each measurement is quantised. A third convention, which trips people up more often than it should, is the numeric encoding: 16-bit signed little-endian integers in the range −32768…32767 is what a WAV file holds, whereas every model you will feed expects floats in roughly −1.0…1.0, so somebody divides by 32768 and if two stages both do it you get audio that is 90 dB down and reads as silence.

The arithmetic is worth carrying in your head because it turns into bandwidth and buffer sizes. Bytes per second is fs × bit_depth/8 × channels. Mono 16 kHz 16-bit is 32,000 B/s, or 256 kbit/s — cheap for one caller, not cheap when you are shipping 500 concurrent legs to a GPU box. A 20 ms frame, the standard RTP packetisation interval, holds 0.020 × fs samples: 160 at 8 kHz, 320 at 16 kHz, 480 at 24 kHz. That the 8 kHz case comes to exactly 160 bytes under G.711's one-byte-per-sample companding is not a coincidence; it is why the packet is that size.

What Nyquist actually forbids

The sampling theorem says a signal containing no energy at or above fs/2 can be reconstructed exactly from its samples by sinc interpolation. The half-rate point is the Nyquist frequency. The part people misremember is the failure case. Energy above Nyquist does not get dropped, and it does not get attenuated. It gets folded: a component at frequency f sampled at fs becomes indistinguishable from one at

f_alias = |f − fs · round(f / fs)|   →   always lands in [0, fs/2]

Sample a 10 kHz tone at 8 kHz and you do not get a faint 10 kHz tone, you get a full-amplitude 2 kHz tone sitting in the middle of the vowel band, with no filter downstream that can remove it because it is now genuinely a 2 kHz component. That is the whole reason an anti-alias low-pass has to run before the decimation, not after.

Why 16 kHz for ASR and 24 kHz for TTS

The rates are not arbitrary, they are two different answers to "what do I need to keep". Recognition needs the cues that discriminate phonemes, and the formants that separate vowels sit below about 3.5 kHz while the fricative energy that separates /s/ from /f/ runs up past 8 kHz but is still classifiable from its lower reaches. 16 kHz gives an 8 kHz ceiling, which covers that, and the convention hardened around it — Whisper's front end takes 16 kHz audio into a log-mel with a 25 ms window and a 10 ms hop, and self-supervised encoders in the wav2vec 2.0 line are trained at the same rate. Synthesis is judged by ear rather than by classifier, and an 8 kHz ceiling makes sibilants sound dull and slightly lisped, so TTS checkpoints commonly ship at 22.05 kHz or 24 kHz — an 11–12 kHz ceiling, enough for /s/ to sound like /s/.

Which means a cascade voice agent is a rate-conversion pipeline whether you designed it that way or not: 8 kHz off the PSTN, up to 16 kHz for the ASR, back down from 24 kHz after the TTS. Three conversions, each of which is a place to fold energy into the wrong band.

Bit depth, companding, and the phone network

For ideal uniform quantisation of a full-scale sinusoid the signal-to-quantisation-noise ratio is 6.02·N + 1.76 dB, so 16-bit buys about 98 dB and 8-bit about 50 dB — and that 50 dB is measured at full scale, so a caller speaking 30 dB down has 20 dB of headroom left and sounds gritty. The telephone network's answer is not more bits but a non-uniform step size: G.711 μ-law spends its 8 bits logarithmically, giving roughly 6.02·N − 10 dB, about 38 dB, but holding that figure roughly constant across a wide range of input levels instead of collapsing for quiet speech. 8 kHz × 8 bits = 64 kbit/s, the number the entire PSTN is built around.

The characteristic failure mode

It is a naive resample, and it sounds like a thin metallic buzz or a shower of descending "birdie" tones that track the speech but do not belong to it. It happens when someone decimates by slicing — x[::3] to get 8 kHz from 24 kHz — with no low-pass first, so everything the source had between 4 and 12 kHz reflects down into 0–4 kHz. It is nastiest in TTS training data because it is inaudible in isolation and catastrophic in aggregate: the mel spectrogram of the aliased audio has spurious low-frequency structure, the acoustic model learns to predict that structure, and the vocoder reproduces it faithfully. The buzz is then baked into the checkpoint and no amount of vocoder retraining removes it. The tell is a spectrogram whose harmonic stack reflects off the Nyquist line instead of fading out at it.

Latency and PHI

Rate conversion is not free in time either. A polyphase FIR resampler with 64 taps running at 16 kHz has a group delay of about half its length, ≈32 samples ≈ 2 ms; the three conversions in a cascade are a few milliseconds of the ~800 ms budget at which a caller starts to hear the pause as dead air. Small, but it is spent before any model has run. And the PCM itself is regulated: in a healthcare setting the raw waveform of a call is PHI, more identifying than the transcript because voice is biometric. Encrypt it at rest, set an explicit retention window, and be deliberate about whether the .wav files you keep for regression testing are inside or outside that boundary.

The two lines that decide whether you get a buzz

# Where a component above Nyquist actually lands.
def fold(f, fs):
    r = f % fs                      # the spectrum repeats every fs
    return r if r <= fs / 2 else fs - r

fold(10_000, 8_000)   # -> 2000.0 Hz, full amplitude, in the vowel band
fold( 3_400, 8_000)   # -> 3400.0 Hz, unharmed: below Nyquist

# WRONG: 24 kHz -> 8 kHz by slicing. Everything the source had
# between 4 and 12 kHz reflects down into 0-4 kHz and stays there.
y = x[::3]

# RIGHT: low-pass below the NEW Nyquist first, then decimate.
# resample_poly applies the anti-alias FIR as part of the polyphase filter.
from scipy.signal import resample_poly
y = resample_poly(x, up=1, down=3)      # 24000 -> 8000
z = resample_poly(x, up=2, down=3)      # 24000 -> 16000 (3:2, not integer)

# PCM budget arithmetic, mono.
def pcm_bytes_per_sec(fs, bit_depth):
    return fs * bit_depth // 8

pcm_bytes_per_sec(16_000, 16)           # -> 32000 B/s = 256 kbit/s
int(0.020 * 8_000) * 1                  # -> 160 B: one 20 ms G.711 RTP payload

The line I'd actually say

"PCM is just amplitudes at a fixed rate, and the only thing the sample rate really decides is your frequency ceiling — half the rate. So 16 kHz for ASR because an 8 kHz ceiling covers the formants and enough fricative energy to classify, and 24 kHz for TTS because you're being judged by ear and a lisped /s/ is audible.

The thing I'd actually watch for on a telephony pipeline is that energy above Nyquist doesn't disappear when you resample, it folds — 10 kHz sampled at 8 lands at 2 kHz, at full amplitude, right in the vowel band. So every rate conversion needs the anti-alias low-pass before the decimation, and if you skip it in the training data the model learns the buzz and the vocoder reproduces it, which you can't fix at serving time."

Takeaways

  • Half the sample rate is the whole story. fs/2 is your frequency ceiling; 8 kHz PSTN gives 4 kHz, 16 kHz ASR gives 8 kHz, 24 kHz TTS gives 12 kHz. Everything else on this page is a consequence.
  • Aliasing folds, it doesn't attenuate. fold(f, fs) = fs − (f mod fs) when the remainder exceeds Nyquist — a 10 kHz component sampled at 8 kHz becomes a full-amplitude 2 kHz component that no downstream filter can separate from real speech.
  • Never decimate by slicing. Use a resampler that low-passes first — scipy.signal.resample_poly, or torchaudio.transforms.Resample if you are already inside PyTorch and want it on the GPU with the rest of the front end.
  • Bit depth buys about 6 dB per bit. 6.02N + 1.76 dB for uniform PCM; G.711 μ-law trades peak SNR (≈38 dB) for level-independence, which is the right trade for a phone line and the reason 64 kbit/s is the PSTN unit.
  • Pin the rate at the pipeline boundary, not in each service. Assert fs and dtype on ingest and on model input the way you would assert a schema; a silent 24→16 kHz mismatch between an ASR model behind Triton and its caller degrades WER without ever raising an error, and exporting the front end to ONNX will not catch it because the tensor shapes still line up.
  • Raw call audio is PHI. The waveform is biometric and more identifying than the transcript — encrypt at rest, set a retention window, and decide explicitly whether golden regression .wav files live inside that boundary.
