10Tacotron 2

The autoregressive encoder–attention–decoder that made neural TTS work — a character or phoneme encoder, location-sensitive attention, a decoder emitting one mel frame at a time, and a stop token deciding when the utterance ends. Everything good and everything fragile about it follows from that one sequential loop.

Theory

The shape

Tacotron 2 maps a character or phoneme sequence to 80-bin mel frames and hands those to a separate vocoder — a modified WaveNet in the paper, HiFi-GAN today. The encoder is a 512-dim embedding, three conv layers of 512 filters with kernel 5 (batch norm, ReLU), then one bidirectional LSTM with 256 units per direction, producing an L×512 memory: one context-aware vector per symbol. The decoder is autoregressive over frames, not symbols.

Attention is the model

Content-based attention asks which encoder state resembles what the decoder needs next; for speech the answer is often "the one I just used, or that other l three characters back." Location-sensitive attention (Chorowski) gives the scorer a memory of where it has been: convolve the cumulative attention weights with 32 filters of width 31, project, and add that to the energy.

e_ij = wᵀ tanh(W·s_{i−1} + V·h_j + U·f_ij + b),   f_i = F ∗ Σ_{k<i} α_k

That U·f term is the difference between speech that reads a sentence and one that stutters. But monotonicity is discouraged, not enforced: nothing forbids a backward jump the way a forced aligner's topology does, which is why this architecture still fails.

One decoder step

The previous mel frame goes through a pre-net — two 256-unit ReLU layers with dropout 0.5 left on at inference; that noise keeps the decoder from locking onto its own predictions. Pre-net output and attention context concatenate into two 1024-unit uni-directional LSTM layers. State and context concatenate again and project twice: to the 80-d mel frame, and to a scalar through a sigmoid — the stop token. Reduction factor is 1: one frame per step, where Tacotron 1 emitted 2–5. A five-layer post-net predicts a residual added to the whole sequence; the loss is MSE before and after it plus BCE on the stop token. With a WaveNet vocoder the paper reported MOS 4.53 against 4.58 for recorded speech.

Where the milliseconds go

At the paper's 12.5 ms hop, one second of audio is 80 decoder steps, so a 1.5 s reply is 120 steps that must run in order. Take 1.5 ms per step as an illustrative figure for an LSTM this small on a GPU — launch-bound, not FLOP-bound — and the utterance costs ~180 ms, RTF near 0.12. Batching other calls raises throughput, not this caller's latency: the dependency is the previous frame.

Time-to-first-audio is far better, and it is what the caller hears. A frame is final only once the post-net can see it: five conv layers at kernel 5 is a receptive field of 21 frames, non-causal, so frame 0 needs frames 1–10 to exist. Those 10 frames represent 125 ms of audio, but producing them is 10 more decoder steps — about 15 ms of wall clock, not 125. First audio therefore lands at 11 steps (17 ms) plus a vocoder chunk (~20 ms): 37 ms. In an 800 ms turn budget where hangover spent ~250 ms, ASR finalisation ~80 ms and LLM time-to-first-token ~300 ms, 37 ms fits the ~170 ms left where 180 ms of full synthesis does not. That gap is the argument for streaming.

The characteristic failure: the stop token never fires

The tail risk lives in one scalar. Fire it early and the utterance truncates mid-word. Fail to fire it — usually because attention collapsed to a diffuse blur and the decoder state never looks like an ending — and generation runs to max_decoder_steps. At the default of 1000 that is 12.5 s of audio and 1.5 s of GPU for a reply that should have cost 180 ms — and what plays is babble, not silence: the context vector is now an average over the encoder, so the mel is a smeared drone the vocoder renders faithfully. Skipped words (a forward jump) and repeated syllables (a backward one) are the same failure read off the same matrix. The answer is a hard step cap, a diagonality check on the emitted attention, and on cap a cached prompt.

On a phone line, with PHI

The mel is bandlimited near 7.6 kHz and a PSTN leg keeps 300–3400 Hz, so much of what this model renders never reaches the caller — while its stop-token tail reaches them in full. Alert on decoder steps per character, not mean latency. Cache fixed prompts, but a readback of a caller's appointment time derives from PHI and inherits its retention rules: key the cache on non-PHI templates only.

Visualization — the decoder loop, frame by frame

# One Tacotron 2 decoder step, r = 1: one 80-bin mel frame per step.
# h: (L, 512) encoder memory.  cum: (L,) running sum of past alignments.
def decode_step(prev_mel, s, ctx, cum, h):
    q    = prenet(prev_mel)                  # 2 x 256 ReLU, dropout 0.5 KEPT ON at inference
    s    = lstm(cat([q, ctx]), s)            # 2 x 1024 uni-directional LSTM

    f    = conv1d(cum, F)                    # 32 filters, width 31 -> location feature
    e    = v @ tanh(W @ s.h + V @ h + U @ f + b)      # (L,) energies
    a    = softmax(e)                        # alignment for THIS frame
    cum  = cum + a                           # the only memory of where we have been
    ctx  = a @ h                             # (512,) context vector

    z    = cat([s.h, ctx])                   # (1536,)
    mel  = W_mel @ z                         # (80,) frame, pre-post-net
    stop = sigmoid(w_stop @ z)               # scalar
    return mel, stop, s, ctx, cum

# Inference is unbounded unless you bound it.
frames = []
for i in range(MAX_DECODER_STEPS):           # 1000 steps = 12.5 s at a 12.5 ms hop
    mel, stop, s, ctx, cum = decode_step(frames[-1] if frames else GO, s, ctx, cum, h)
    frames.append(mel)
    if stop > 0.5: break
else:
    metrics.incr("tts.stop_token_never_fired")   # what plays is babble, not silence

mels = stack(frames)
mels = mels + postnet(mels)   # 5 conv layers, k=5 -> RF 21 frames, NON-causal.

The line I'd actually say

"Tacotron 2 is encoder, attention, autoregressive decoder. Conv stack plus a BiLSTM gives you one vector per character; the decoder emits one 80-bin mel frame per step, conditioned on the previous frame through a pre-net that keeps its dropout on at inference; and a post-net adds a residual over the finished sequence. The part that actually does the work is location-sensitive attention — the cumulative alignment convolved with 32 width-31 filters is fed into the energy, so the model knows where it has already been. That's what makes the alignment come out diagonal."

"What I'd flag in production is that it's sequential and it's unbounded. At a 12.5 ms hop, a 1.5 s reply is 120 steps you can't parallelise — call it 180 ms of compute, RTF around 0.12 — and batching helps throughput, not this caller. Time-to-first-audio is much better: the post-net's 21-frame receptive field is non-causal, so the first frame isn't final until ten more exist — but that is ten decoder steps, about 15 ms of wall clock, even though those frames carry 125 ms of audio. First audio lands under 40 ms. And the tail: if attention smears, the stop token never fires and you run to max_decoder_steps — 1000 steps is 12.5 seconds of babble down the phone. So a hard cap, a diagonality check, and a cached fallback prompt aren't optional. That unbounded tail is most of the reason people move to FastSpeech 2 for telephony."

Takeaways

  • Location-sensitive attention is the whole trick. The cumulative alignment convolved with 32 filters of width 31 enters the energy as U·f, so the scorer knows where it has been. Monotonicity is discouraged, not enforced — nothing in the graph forbids a backward jump, unlike a forced aligner's topology.
  • One frame per step, in order. 12.5 ms hop → 80 steps per second of audio; a 1.5 s reply is 120 sequential steps, ~180 ms at an illustrative 1.5 ms/step on GPU, RTF ~0.12. The step is launch-bound, so Triton dynamic batching raises concurrency and never shortens one caller's utterance.
  • Time-to-first-audio, not total time, is what the caller hears. The post-net is 5 conv layers at kernel 5 — receptive field 21 frames, non-causal — so the first frame is final only once ten more exist. Those ten frames carry 125 ms of audio but cost ~15 ms of wall clock: first audio lands near 37 ms while the full 1.5 s utterance still takes ~180 ms.
  • The stop token is the tail risk. Early → truncation mid-word; never → run to max_decoder_steps, 12.5 s of babble at the usual 1000. Cap it, score alignment diagonality per utterance, export the steps-per-character distribution to Prometheus, and fall back to a pre-rendered prompt on cap.
  • It is a mel model, not an audio model. Quality on the wire is bounded by the vocoder and then by the 300–3400 Hz PSTN leg; the paper pairs it with a modified WaveNet and NVIDIA's reference PyTorch implementation with WaveGlow, but anything shipping on a phone line pairs it with HiFi-GAN for the RTF.
  • Cache on templates, not on text. Fixed prompts synthesise once and replay from Redis or a blob store; audio that reads back a caller's appointment or member ID is derived from PHI and inherits its retention and access rules.
