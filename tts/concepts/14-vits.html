14VITS and End-to-End TTS

One model, one training run, phonemes to waveform. A conditional VAE whose prior is sharpened by a normalizing flow, aligned by dynamic programming instead of attention, decoded by a HiFi-GAN generator against a multi-period discriminator, with durations sampled rather than regressed. The mel spectrogram survives only as a loss term — and with it goes the seam you used to debug through.

Theory

What “end-to-end” actually deletes

Every architecture so far has a seam in the middle: text in, a mel spectrogram out, and a separately trained vocoder turning that mel into a waveform. The seam is useful: you can look at the mel, swap the vocoder, train the halves on different corpora. It is also a mismatch. The vocoder is trained on ground-truth mel and served predicted mel, so at inference it meets a distribution it never saw. VITS (Kim, Kong and Son, ICML 2021 — Variational Inference with adversarial learning for end-to-end Text-to-Speech) removes the seam. Phonemes to 22.05 kHz waveform in one model, trained once, nothing handed between components at inference.

A conditional VAE with a flow on the prior

The latent z is a frame-rate representation of the audio, not of the text. A posterior encoder — non-causal WaveNet residual blocks — reads the linear-scale log-magnitude spectrogram of the target speech, not a mel, and emits a mean and variance per frame from which z is sampled. At 22.05 kHz with a 256-sample hop, one latent frame is 11.61 ms, so a 1.6 s utterance is about 139 frames.

The prior has to be conditioned on text, and a plain per-phoneme Gaussian is too weak for a waveform latent. So VITS pushes z through a normalizing flow fθ and matches the transformed latent z_p to the text-conditioned Gaussian instead. The flow is a stack of affine coupling layers deliberately built to be volume-preserving — Jacobian determinant one — so the change-of-variables term vanishes and the KL reduces to two Gaussian log-densities. The decoder is the HiFi-GAN V1 generator, taking z straight to samples.

Alignment by dynamic programming, not attention

There is no attention between text and frames. Alignment is a monotonic, surjective map found by Monotonic Alignment Search (MAS), inherited from Glow-TTS: a dynamic program over the phoneme × frame grid picking the alignment that maximises the ELBO. Because the reconstruction term does not depend on the alignment, that reduces to maximising the log-likelihood of z_p under the aligned text prior. Skipping, repeating and non-terminating babble are structurally impossible, as in FastSpeech 2 — but without an external aligner, because the alignment is re-estimated inside the training loop against the model's own prior. The residual risk moves to warm-up: early in training the prior is meaningless, MAS latches onto a poor path, and the model can settle into a wrong segmentation no later epoch dislodges.

Durations are sampled, not predicted

Column sums of the MAS alignment give per-phoneme durations, and those train a duration predictor — but not a regressor. FastSpeech 2's MSE on log-duration learns the conditional mean, so a sentence has exactly one rhythm forever. VITS models the duration distribution with a second flow of monotonic rational-quadratic spline coupling layers, trained on a variational lower bound, with dequantization and augmentation because durations are discrete scalars. At inference you draw noise, run that flow backwards, ceil to integers, and length-regulate the prior. The same sentence gets a different rhythm on every call. That is the feature in a conversational agent and the problem in a regression harness.

Adversarial training and the windowed decoder

Five losses: L1 between the mel of the generated waveform and the mel of the target (the mel returns as a loss, never as an interface), the KL above, the duration bound, and HiFi-GAN's adversarial and feature-matching losses against a multi-period discriminator. The adversarial term is least-squares (LSGAN), not hinge and not vanilla cross-entropy: the discriminator minimises E[(D(y)−1)²] + E[D(G(z))²] and the generator E[(D(G(z))−1)²]. Backpropagating through a whole utterance of waveform does not fit in memory, so the decoder is trained on random 32-frame slices of z — about 372 ms — with the discriminator shown the matching slice of real audio. The generator sees the full sequence only at inference. A speaker embedding conditions the posterior encoder, flow, decoder and duration predictor for multi-speaker.

What it costs

The paper reports a MOS of 4.43 for VITS against 4.46 for ground-truth recordings on LJSpeech — their raters, their test set, not a promise about yours. The bill is paid elsewhere. First, debuggability: when output buzzes you cannot tell which half did it, because there is no mel to inspect and no vocoder to swap. The answer is a retrain, not a component change. Second, you inherit GAN training dynamics on the critical path of a product. Third, the failures split by half. An undertrained adversarial half does not babble — it buzzes and smears in HiFi-GAN's voice, because the decoder is HiFi-GAN. An undertrained prior or flow gives correct rhythm carrying mushy or wrong phones. And with the noise scales turned up you get this architecture's signature artefact: a fluent read in which one phone is drawled several times too long, because a single unlucky draw from the duration flow has nothing downstream to smooth it.

On a call

VITS emits nothing until the decoder has run over the whole latent sequence, so time-to-first-audio is full synthesis time. Say your measured RTF is 0.05 — illustrative; measure your own hardware. In an 800 ms turn budget with 250 ms endpointing hangover, 100 ms ASR finalisation, 300 ms LLM time-to-first-token and 50 ms of network, you have roughly 100 ms left. A 4 s answer costs 200 ms and blows it; the first 0.8 s clause costs 40 ms and fits. So you chunk at clause boundaries and cross-fade, and each chunk redraws its own durations. The decoder's upsample stack (8×8×2×2 = 256) builds 22,050 samples a second; a PSTN leg band-limits near 3.4 kHz and resamples to 8 kHz mu-law — 2.76× more samples than the caller can hear, which argues for a narrowband decoder. Two compliance notes: the posterior encoder trains directly on caller spectrograms, so PHI ends up distributed across one inseparable set of weights with no intermediate you can scrub; and the multi-speaker embedding is a voice identity vector, which carries consent and disclosure obligations of its own.

Visualization — two graphs, and a duration you draw

# ---- TRAINING: one objective, no mel handed between components -------------
z, mu_q, sig_q = posterior_encoder(x_lin)      # LINEAR spec -> latent, 11.61 ms/frame
z_p            = flow(z)                       # affine coupling, volume-preserving
mu_p, sig_p    = text_encoder(phonemes)        # prior, one (mu, sigma) per phoneme
A              = monotonic_alignment_search(z_p, mu_p, sig_p)   # DP, monotonic by construction

L_kl   = log_q(z, mu_q, sig_q) - log_p(z_p, expand(mu_p, sig_p, A))  # log|det J| == 0, drops out
z_s    = slice(z, 32)                          # 32 frames ~= 372 ms; whole utterance won't fit
L_rec  = L1(mel(decoder(z_s)), mel(x_slice))   # the ONLY place a mel appears
L_adv  = mean((mpd(decoder(z_s)) - 1)**2) + feature_match(...)  # LSGAN; D: (D(y)-1)**2 + D(G(z))**2
L_dur  = -variational_bound(spline_flow, A.sum(axis=1), h_text) # duration DISTRIBUTION, not its mean

# ---- INFERENCE: posterior encoder and discriminator are not in the graph ----
d      = ceil(exp(sdp_inverse(randn(L) * sigma_w, h_text)))     # sampled; sigma_w = noise_scale_w
mu, sg = expand(mu_p, sig_p, d)                # length regulate to T = sum(d) frames
z_p    = mu + sg * randn(T) * tau              # tau = noise_scale (timbre, not rhythm)
wav    = decoder(flow_inverse(z_p))            # 22050 Hz in one parallel pass

# 22050 / 256 -> 11.61 ms per frame. T = 139 frames -> 1.614 s of audio.
# TTFA = whole-utterance synthesis: 1.614 s * RTF -- nothing streams early.

The line I'd actually say

"VITS collapses the acoustic model and the vocoder into one conditional VAE. A posterior encoder reads the linear spectrogram and gives you a frame-rate latent; the text encoder gives you a prior over that latent per phoneme; a volume-preserving normalizing flow sits between them so the prior is expressive enough to match, and Monotonic Alignment Search does the alignment by dynamic programming instead of attention. The decoder is just HiFi-GAN, trained adversarially on 32-frame slices of the latent. The mel never leaves the model — it only shows up as an L1 reconstruction loss."

"The bit I'd actually flag is the duration predictor, because it's a flow, not a regressor — you sample durations rather than predict their mean, so the same sentence has a different rhythm on every call. That's the prosodic variety FastSpeech 2 flattens, and it's also why a golden regression test has to assert on the transcript and the timing distribution, never on waveform bytes. And what you're really trading away is the seam: when it buzzes, there's no mel to look at and no vocoder to swap, so debugging is a retrain. On telephony I'd still chunk it at clause boundaries — nothing streams out until the whole latent is decoded, so a four-second answer at RTF 0.05 is 200 ms of time-to-first-audio and that doesn't fit the ~100 ms a TTS stage gets in an 800 ms turn."

Takeaways

  • One model, no interface. Conditional VAE: posterior encoder on the linear spectrogram, text-conditioned prior, a volume-preserving flow between them so the KL has no log-determinant term, HiFi-GAN generator on the latent. The mel appears once, as an L1 loss. In PyTorch that is a single training loop with five loss terms, not two stages.
  • MAS replaces attention, and replaces the aligner too. A monotonic dynamic program over the phoneme×frame grid, re-run each step against the model's own prior — so no Montreal Forced Aligner dependency, but a real risk that a bad warm-up alignment sets and never moves.
  • Durations are drawn from a spline flow, not regressed. That is the whole prosody argument: MSE gives you the conditional mean and one rhythm per sentence; sampling gives you variation the ear reads as human. Fix the seed in CI, and assert on ASR transcript plus timing distribution rather than bytes.
  • Failure splits by half, and neither half babbles. Adversarial side undertrained → HiFi-GAN buzz and smear; prior or flow undertrained → right rhythm, mushy phones; noise scales too high → one phone drawled absurdly. Export per-utterance frame counts and duration variance to Prometheus so a drifting noise_scale_w is visible before a caller hears it.
  • End-to-end costs you the debugging seam. No mel to inspect, no vocoder to swap, no way to A/B one half; a fix is a retrain. Weigh that against the mismatch the seam creates in a cascade — it is a real trade, not a strict improvement.
  • Telephony changes the arithmetic. Nothing emits until the full latent is decoded, so TTFA is whole-utterance synthesis — chunk at clauses. And a 22.05 kHz decoder feeding an 8 kHz mu-law leg builds 2.76× the samples the caller can hear; a narrowband decoder, exported through ONNX and served on Triton, is the cheaper shape.
