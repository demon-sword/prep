33Audio-LM-as-Judge Failure Modes

An audio language model asked to judge styled speech agrees with human raters about 60% of the time — and fine-tuning it on judge labels does not fix it. The failure is not a training-data shortage. It is an acoustic grounding gap: a text-shaped prior scoring waveforms it cannot really hear.

Theory

Take a fixed eval suite — 16 steering directions (“whisper”, “brisk confirmation”, “empathetic”) — and ask both a human panel and an audio LM the same pairwise question: which of these two renders follows the direction better? Pairwise agreement is the fraction of pairs where judge and panel pick the same winner. The reported figure is roughly 60%: on 120 pairs the judge matches the panel on about 72. That sounds only mildly bad until you place the baselines. Chance is 50%, so 60% is barely above coin-flip; a second human panel typically agrees in the high 70s to 80s. The judge is much closer to random than to a second human.

Report it honestly: agreement on n pairs is a binomial proportion, so quote the Wilson interval with it. At 72/120 the 95% interval spans roughly ±9 points — the “true” agreement could be anywhere from the low 50s to the high 60s. Anyone who reports 60% without n is reporting noise with confidence.

Confusion modes: what the judge hears wrong

The errors are not uniform; they cluster on exactly the acoustic distinctions a phone call already degrades. Low pitch versus low volume is the first confusion: both lower the signal in overlapping ways — a dropped F0 mean and a dropped energy floor both read as “quieter, flatter” to a model whose acoustic front end never learned to separate source (pitch) from gain (loudness). Ask whether a render is “low-pitched” and the judge fires on quiet clips; ask whether it is “soft” and it fires on low-pitched ones. The second mode is hallucinated inaudible traits: the judge asserts breathiness, room tone or warmth that no listener hears — sometimes where the trait is physically absent. That is text-prior confabulation: the model knows that “empathetic” should be breathy, so it reports breathiness whether or not the waveform contains it. Hallucinated traits correlate with the direction prompt's wording, not with measured F0, energy or tilt — the verdict follows the text, not the audio.

Why fine-tuning the judge does not transfer

The obvious repair — collect a few hundred human preference pairs and fine-tune the audio judge — lifts agreement on held-out pairs from the same directions and voices, then collapses back toward 60% on anything new: a 17th direction, a new speaker, a fine-tuning adjusts the text-side head far more than the audio encoder: “A beats B” labels supervise wording and direction semantics, which the text tower already represents, while the encoder that would need to learn pitch/volume disentanglement gets almost no usable signal from them. Second, prior dominance: the base model spent orders of magnitude more training on text than on waveform-level discrimination, so under uncertainty it defaults to the text prior — the hallucinated-trait mode survives fine-tuning intact. Third, suite overfitting: a few hundred pairs over 16 fixed directions teach the judge the suite, not the skill — which direction wordings beat which, on which voices. Text-judge fine-tuning transfers further only because a text judge's input modality is its strong one, so the labels refine an already-grounded representation.

Calibrate, don't fine-tune

Treat the judge the way objective metrics are treated: a cheap proxy with a calibrated operating point, not a rater. Collect a golden set of human-labelled pairs per direction, score them with the judge, and fit a per-direction accept threshold plus an abstain band: pairs the judge scores above the high threshold auto-pass, below the low threshold auto-fail, and everything between goes to a human — fit per direction, because a global threshold lets the easily-judged directions subsidise the confused ones. At 60% agreement with a real abstain band, roughly a third of eval traffic still needs ears. Track the whole thing the way concept 32 tracks golden calls — judge version, thresholds and golden-set hash pinned together, so a judge swap that shifts scores fails the regression instead of silently re-grading. And evaluate open-vocabulary steering on unseen directions only: re-running the same 16 fixed directions after a steering change measures suite memory, per concept 27. 

pairs = load_golden(direction)          # human winner per pair, fixed suite
agree = mean(judge_pick(a, b) == human_pick(a, b) for a, b in pairs)
lo, hi = wilson_interval(agree, n=len(pairs))   # 60% on n=120: ~[0.51, 0.68]

# Per-direction operating point, not one global threshold:
thr_hi, thr_lo = fit_abstain_band(judge_scores(pairs), human_labels(pairs))
#   score > thr_hi: auto-pass; score < thr_lo: auto-fail; else: human.
# Refit per judge version; pin (judge_ckpt, thr, golden_hash) as one artifact.

The line I'd actually say

“An audio LM judging styled speech agrees with a human panel about 60% of the time on pairwise comparisons — chance is 50 and a second human panel is up in the high 70s, so the judge is closer to random than to a rater. The errors cluster: it confuses low pitch with low volume, and it hallucinates traits like breathiness that correlate with the direction wording rather than the waveform. And fine-tuning on preference pairs doesn't transfer — it learns the 16 fixed directions in the suite, then falls back to 60 the moment you add a 17th direction or a new voice.”

“So I'd never fine-tune-and-trust it. I'd pin a golden set per direction, fit an accept threshold with an abstain band that routes the uncertain middle to humans, and version the judge checkpoint, the thresholds and the golden-set hash as one artifact — the same way I'd gate a speaker-cosine threshold. At 60% agreement you're buying a triage stage, not a rater, and the budget line is the human-review share, not the model call.”

Takeaways

  • 60% agreement is barely above chance, not nearly human. Pairwise judge–panel agreement of ~60% sits between the 50% coin-flip floor and high-70s panel–panel agreement. Always quote it with n and the Wilson interval — 72/120 spans roughly ±9 points.
  • Errors cluster on pitch/volume confusion and hallucinated traits. Low pitch fires the “soft” detector and quiet clips fire “low-pitched”; breathiness and warmth track the direction wording, not measured F0, energy or tilt. Correlate verdicts against acoustics before trusting any trait score.
  • Fine-tuning teaches the suite, not the skill. Preference pairs supervise the text tower, not the audio encoder, so agreement lifts on held-out same-suite pairs and collapses on a new direction, speaker or codec leg. Evaluate steering changes on unseen directions only.
  • Fit per-direction thresholds with an abstain band, and pin the triple. Judge checkpoint, threshold artifact and golden-set hash ship together — thresholds tracked in Prometheus, artifacts versioned in MLflow — so a judge-model swap that shifts scores fails the regression instead of silently re-grading. Monitor drift with Evidently.
  • Budget ears, not just calls. At 60% agreement with a real abstain band, roughly a third of eval traffic needs human review; that review capacity is the cost line. Export the judge to ONNX for cheap local scoring, but never let cheap scoring argue for zero humans.
