21Barge-In and Interruption Detection

Letting the caller talk over the agent is three separate problems wearing one name: noticing them through your own outbound audio, deciding whether “mhm” was a request for the floor, and then repairing the conversation state so the model does not believe it said something the caller never heard.

Theory

Two clocks, and the gap between them

Barge-in is hard because “now” means two different times. Your media path writes RTP packets paced in real time; the caller hears each one an outbound leg later — transit plus the far-end jitter buffer, call it 65 ms. Their speech reaches you an inbound leg later — packetisation, transit, your jitter buffer, call it 75 ms. So when the caller opens their mouth you are already 75 ms from knowing it, and every sample already on the socket keeps arriving in their ear for another 65 ms. Those 140 ms are the floor, and a barge-in latency that excludes them describes your process, not the call.

Detect, duck, then confirm

Cancelling on the first voiced frame gives an agent that abandons its sentence when someone clears their throat; waiting for certainty gives one that monologues over a caller correcting an appointment date. Use two triggers at different speeds. The fast one is acoustic: a VAD on the inbound stream — Silero's 16 kHz model consumes 512-sample (32 ms) chunks, WebRTC's classic VAD takes 10, 20 or 30 ms frames — where three consecutive voiced 20 ms frames is 60 ms of evidence. On that you duck rather than cancel: attenuate the outbound TTS by 9–12 dB and keep synthesising. Ducking is reversible, and it is what a person does when they think they are being interrupted. The slow trigger is lexical: at roughly 280 ms of sustained speech you read the ASR partial, and only then commit — ducking buys those 220 ms.

The cost lands in one line: talk-over = IN + commit + OUT. At 75 + 280 + 65 that is 420 ms of agent still audible after the caller started — brisk enough to read as yielding the floor rather than ignoring it.

What a cancel actually reaches

Four things are in flight: LLM tokens still decoding, TTS chunks queued behind them, synthesised PCM in your playout buffer, and RTP already on the wire. Aborting the LLM and the vocoder stops the producers, and almost every first implementation stops there — the playout buffer drains happily into the caller's ear. Pre-buffer 400 ms against underruns, forget to flush it, and that is 400 ms of extra talk-over: jitter insurance from the streaming design, charged straight back here. Flush it, and budget for what you cannot — the OUT milliseconds past the socket, which play regardless.

Backchannel, cough, or a real interruption

“Mhm”, “yeah” and “right” tell you to continue; treating them as interruptions gives an agent that cannot finish a sentence in front of an engaged listener. Duration alone half-works, since most are short, but a drawn-out “yeaaah, but—” is both. Combine three signals: sustained voiced duration, a lexical check of the ASR partial against a small backchannel set, and an override that commits anyway past about 700 ms, since a long backchannel is a real turn attempt. Third case: a cough or a handset scraping a desk, energetic enough to pass any VAD and caught only by requiring one confident word in the partial.

Truncating the context to what was heard

This step gets skipped, and it is the one with clinical consequences. The LLM generated twenty-six words, the vocoder rendered maybe eighteen, the caller heard eleven. Append all twenty-six and the model believes it delivered “do not eat anything after midnight” — so it will not repeat it, and the stored transcript, part of the record in a healthcare deployment, asserts something that never reached the patient. Map played audio back to text with the alignment you already own: FastSpeech 2's duration predictor gives word boundaries directly, an autoregressive model recovers them from the attention path, a streaming vocoder gives chunk boundaries at least. Truncate at the last word ending before the wire pointer, and mark the turn interrupted.

Err short. Assume the caller heard more than they did and the agent silently drops information; assume less and it repeats a clause. For a dose, a fasting instruction or an appointment time, repetition is the cheap error and omission the expensive one — subtract a safety margin, never add one.

The failure mode with a sound

Self-barge-in. If echo cancellation is absent or has diverged, your own voice returns on the inbound leg one round trip later and your VAD, doing exactly its job, reports speech. The agent cancels itself about OUT + IN + commit milliseconds into its first sentence, restarts, and cancels again — a stutter that decays into silence. Note what does not save you: the lexical gate makes it worse, because echo is grammatical speech and the backchannel filter waves it through. Only acoustic echo cancellation fixes echo, so watch ERLE rather than the transcript — with outbound speech near −20 dBov against a −45 dBov VAD floor you need better than 25 dB of echo return loss enhancement before the leak stops firing. The sibling bug is clipping the caller's first syllable, because you opened the ASR at the trigger: keep a 300 ms lookback ring and feed it the pre-trigger audio.

The honest speech-to-speech comparison

Full-duplex models — Moshi's parallel input and output streams are the clearest published example — listen while they generate, so interruption handling lives inside the model rather than in your scheduler. What goes with the scheduler is the explicit cancel point: that ugly seam is where the cascade truncates the transcript to what was heard, logs interrupted, and runs the PHI filter. A model emitting audio continuously gives no such boundary, and often no report of what was played. A real trade, not a settled one.

The scheduler, written out

# Two-stage barge-in. Duck fast and reversibly; commit slowly and once.
BACKCHANNEL = {"mhm", "uh huh", "yeah", "ok", "okay", "right", "sure", "yep"}
LOOKBACK_MS = 300   # pre-trigger ring, or you clip their first syllable
SAFETY_MS   = 150   # err SHORT: repeat a clause, never drop one

def on_inbound_frame(f, st):
    # f.pcm has already passed AEC. Feeding the raw mic here is the
    # self-interrupt bug: your own voice returns one round trip later.
    if not vad(f.pcm):
        st.voiced_ms = 0
        if st.ducked and st.silence_ms >= 200: unduck()  # backchannel: floor back
        return
    st.voiced_ms += f.ms
    if st.voiced_ms >= st.duck_frames * 20 and not st.ducked:
        duck(-12)                          # attenuate, keep synthesising
    if st.ducked and st.voiced_ms >= st.commit_ms:
        p = asr.partial(preroll=st.ring(LOOKBACK_MS)).text.strip().lower()
        if st.lexical_gate:
            if p in BACKCHANNEL and st.voiced_ms < 700: return   # keep talking
            if not p: return                                     # cough, not words
        cancel_turn(st)

def cancel_turn(st):
    llm.abort(); tts.abort()          # stops the PRODUCERS only
    st.playout.flush()                # omit this line = +buffer ms of talk-over
    sent = st.playout.written_to_wire_ms   # on the wire == will reach the ear
    # talk_over = inbound + commit + outbound (+ buffer if you skipped the flush)
    heard = st.alignment.words_ending_before(sent - SAFETY_MS)
    st.history.append({"role": "assistant", "content": " ".join(heard) + " —",
                       "interrupted": True, "spoken_ms": sent})

The line I'd actually say

“I'd split it into detect, cancel, and repair. Detection is two-stage, because a single threshold can't be both fast and right: three voiced VAD frames — about 60 ms — and I duck the outbound audio by 10 dB, which is reversible; then around 280 ms of sustained speech I check the ASR partial against a backchannel list and only then commit. Ducking is what buys me those 220 ms.”

“Cancel is where people ship the bug: aborting the LLM and the vocoder stops the producers, but the playout buffer keeps draining, so if I pre-buffered 400 ms for jitter I talk over the caller for 400 ms longer than I think. What the caller actually experiences is inbound leg plus commit threshold plus outbound leg — 75 plus 280 plus 65, about 420 ms.”

“Then repair, which is the part that matters clinically: truncate the assistant turn to the words whose audio reached the wire, using the TTS alignment, minus a safety margin — err short, because an agent that repeats ‘nothing after midnight’ is annoying and one that believes it already said it is a patient-safety problem. And if the agent interrupts itself, that's echo, not policy: check ERLE before you touch any threshold.”

Takeaways

  • Duck fast, commit slow. Attenuate the outbound stream by 9–12 dB after ~60 ms of voiced frames, then confirm at ~280 ms against the ASR partial. Ducking is reversible, so it is the only decision worth making on 60 ms of evidence.
  • Talk-over is IN + commit + OUT. 75 + 280 + 65 ≈ 420 ms. Abort the LLM and vocoder but forget the playout flush and you add its full depth — the jitter protection from the streaming design bills you back here.
  • Truncate the history to what reached the wire, and err short. Use the TTS word alignment, subtract a safety margin, and record interrupted: true. Omission is the expensive error for a dose or a fasting instruction; repetition is the cheap one.
  • Self-barge-in is an echo bug wearing a policy costume. With outbound speech near −20 dBov against a −45 dBov VAD floor you need >25 dB of ERLE; below that the agent cancels itself one round trip in and stutters. The lexical gate makes it worse, since echo is grammatical speech.
  • Instrument the caller's frame, not yours. Export talk-over duration, duck-to-commit reversals and self-cancel counts to Prometheus, alarm on p95 in Grafana, keep per-call turn state and the alignment map in Redis, and run the VAD as ONNX on the media node rather than round-tripping to Triton — a 20 ms hop per frame is the whole duck budget.
  • Full-duplex speech-to-speech removes the scheduler, and the seam with it. Handling interruptions inside the model deletes the round trip — and the explicit cancel point where the cascade truncates the transcript, logs the interruption and runs the PHI filter. Live trade, not a solved one.
