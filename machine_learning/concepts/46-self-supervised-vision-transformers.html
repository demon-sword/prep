46Self-Supervised Vision Transformers

MAE-first tour of ViT pre-training without labels: masked reconstruction (MAE, BEiT), self-distillation (DINO), unified latent prediction (data2vec), DINOv2 scale, and the contrastive CNN baselines they displaced.

Theory

Why masking beats augmenting for ViTs#mae

Contrastive methods taught CNNs with hand-designed augmentations — crop, recolor, blur — and asked the network to recognize two views of the same image as the same instance. That works, but the augmentations are domain folklore, and convolutions need dense grids, so nothing can ever be truly hidden. Patches changed the game: a ViT sees an image as a set of tokens, so you can delete most of them and the architecture still runs. Masked modeling ports the BERT playbook to vision, with one decisive difference: language tokens are dense with meaning, while image patches are heavily redundant. BERT masks 15%; vision can mask 75% and the reconstruction task stays solvable — and hard enough to force real understanding.

MAE (He et al., 2021) is the cleanest statement of the idea and the highest-yield mechanism in this whole page. Randomly mask 75% of the patches, then:

  1. Encode only the visible 25%. The large ViT encoder never sees mask tokens at all — no wasted compute on blanks. Since self-attention is quadratic in sequence length, encoding 49 tokens instead of 196 cuts encoder FLOPs to roughly a sixteenth per layer, yielding the paper's headline 3×-or-more wall-clock pre-training speedup.
  2. Decode with a small separate decoder. A lightweight decoder (default: 8 blocks, 512 wide — about 10% of encoder compute per token) takes the encoded visible patches plus learned mask tokens and reconstructs the missing pixels.
  3. Loss on masked patches only — mean-squared error against per-patch-normalized pixels. Predicting only what was hidden keeps the network from wasting capacity on copying visible content.

The result: a ViT-Huge pre-trained with no labels reaches 87.8% top-1 on ImageNet-1K after fine-tuning, and the representations transfer strongly to detection and segmentation. The interview-ready summary is the asymmetry — big encoder on little data, little decoder on the reconstruction task, loss only where information was removed — plus the ratio argument: 75% works because images are redundant; the same ratio on text would destroy the sentence.

# MAE in one screen (PyTorch-flavoured pseudocode)
visible, masked_ids = random_mask(patches, ratio=0.75)
latent  = encoder(visible)              # heavy ViT, only 25% of tokens
full    = interleave(latent, mask_tokens, masked_ids)
pixels  = lightweight_decoder(full)     # tiny ViT, full length
loss    = mse(pixels[masked_ids], norm(patches[masked_ids]))
# fine-tune: discard the decoder, keep the encoder

BEiT as the contrast: discrete tokens vs pixels#beit-vs-mae

BEiT (Bao et al., 2021) is the BERT-faithful foil to MAE: instead of regressing raw pixels, it predicts discrete visual tokens for the masked patches — a classification objective over a learned vocabulary, exactly like masked-language modeling. The vocabulary comes from a dVAE image tokenizer (the DALL-E tokenizer, ~8k codes) that must itself be pre-trained, and masking uses blockwise patterns at around 40% rather than MAE's uniform 75%.

The tradeoff is worth internalizing because interviewers probe it. Discrete tokens give a stable classification loss and a BERT-like training recipe (BEiT even initializes from the same codebase patterns as language pre-training), but they inherit the tokenizer's ceiling: reconstruction quality, and hence the supervision signal, can never exceed what the frozen dVAE can represent, and you pay for a whole separate tokenizer-training stage. MAE's pixel regression needs no tokenizer and scales cleanly, at the cost of spending capacity on high-frequency pixel detail that no downstream task wants. History voted MAE: later state-of-the-art recipes (including DINOv2's masked branch) predict in latent space rather than either extreme.

DINO: the teacher that is yourself#dino

DINO (Caron et al., 2021) drops reconstruction entirely and distills augmented views into each other. A student network sees aggressive crops of an image; a teacher network — an exponential moving average of the student, updated with momentum and never trained by gradient — sees gentler global views. The student is trained to match the teacher's output distribution (cross-entropy between sharpened softmaxes). No labels, no negatives.

Two mechanisms prevent the classic self-supervised collapse where every image maps to the same vector. Centering subtracts a running mean from the teacher outputs, which forbids one dimension from dominating; sharpening (a low teacher temperature) keeps the target distribution peaky, which forbids the uniform solution. Together they pin the dynamics away from both degenerate fixed points — a two-line trick that replaces the entire negative-sample machinery of contrastive learning.

DINO's other signature is multi-crop: 2 large global crops plus several small local crops (the paper's default is 8 at 96×96), with local-to-global matching forcing the network to relate parts to wholes. Out of this come the famous emergent properties: DINO ViT attention maps segment objects with no supervision, kNN classification on frozen features nearly matches linear probing, and the momentum teacher consistently outperforms the student it was built from — evidence the averaging itself is doing useful ensembling, not just bookkeeping.

Scale footnotes: DINOv2 and data2vec#dinov2 #data2vec

DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) is what happens when DINO is engineered for scale rather than elegance. Training moves to LVD-142M — 142 million curated images — with a 1B-scale ViT-giant, and the loss becomes a joint objective: the DINO self-distillation term plus an iBOT masked-modeling term that reconstructs masked patch representations (not pixels, not discrete tokens — latent features, the middle path MAE and BEiT pointed at). Add serious data curation, regularization (KoLeo), and high-resolution fine-tuning, and the payoff is frozen features that beat many fine-tuned baselines: linear probes on DINOv2 backbones became the default strong baseline for downstream vision work. DINOv2 is also where the register story lives — these giant models are exactly the ones that grew high-norm artifact tokens, fixed by the register tokens on page 45.

data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022) generalizes the teacher-student idea across modalities with one framework for vision, speech, and text. The student sees a masked input; the teacher sees the full input and produces contextualized latent targets — the average of its top-K layer outputs, so the target for each masked position blends local detail with whole-input context. The student regresses those latents. Masking features instead of pixels (or waveforms, or tokens) is the unifying move: the same loss trains a ViT, a wav2vec-style speech net, and a BERT. data2vec 2.0 keeps the framework and re-engineers the speed: an efficient decoder design and masking strategy cut training time dramatically, answering the main practical complaint about the original. Treat both as the "masked-latent-prediction" corner of the design space — supervision signal richer than pixels (MAE), cheaper than a frozen tokenizer (BEiT).

Contrastive background: the baselines they displaced#contrastive-background

Every method above was measured against the contrastive CNN era, so keep four names straight. SimCLR showed augmentations plus a projection head plus the NT-Xent loss over huge batches (4096) could learn strong features — with the batch itself supplying negatives. MoCo v2 replaced the giant batch with a momentum-updated queue (dictionary) of past keys, decoupling negative count from batch size. BYOL proved negatives were optional: a predictor head plus a momentum target network, with batch normalization quietly preventing collapse. SwAV swapped instance discrimination for online clustering — match cluster assignments between views instead of matching instances, with multi-crop doing heavy lifting. The arc from SimCLR to DINO is the field learning that the collapse-avoidance mechanism (negatives → queues → predictor → centering/sharpening) matters more than the pretext task, and that ViT token structure unlocks masking as the strongest pretext of all.

The interview line

MAE first: mask 75%, encode only the visible quarter, reconstruct pixels with a tiny decoder — the asymmetry is the whole trick. BEiT is the same idea through a discrete tokenizer. DINO replaces reconstruction with self-distillation against a momentum teacher, held away from collapse by centering plus sharpening, and multi-crop gives it segmentation for free. DINOv2 scales that recipe to 142M images with a masked-latent term, and data2vec unifies it across modalities by predicting contextualized latents.

Key Takeaways

  • Mask ratio is a redundancy dial, not a magic number: 75% works for pixels because neighboring patches are predictable, 40% blockwise suits BEiT's discrete codes, and 15% suits text — quote the ratio with the modality argument or the number is trivia.
  • MAE's asymmetry is the production lesson: encoding only visible patches makes pre-training 3× cheaper, so in PyTorch or Hugging Face transformers pipelines always check whether masked positions can skip the heavy encoder rather than ride along as zero-vectors.
  • DINO's centering-plus-sharpening pair replaced an entire negatives infrastructure: when a self-supervised run collapses, diagnose which degenerate fixed point you hit (uniform vs one-hot) before reaching for bigger batches or FAISS-mined hard negatives.
  • Frozen-feature evaluation is the honest metric: linear probes in scikit-learn and kNN search over frozen backbones separate representation quality from fine-tuning skill — DINOv2 made this the default comparison, and any SSL claim without one deserves skepticism.
  • Defaults for new work: start from a DINOv2 backbone in timm with registers enabled for dense tasks, reach for MAE-style masked pre-training when you have unlabeled domain images, and reserve contrastive baselines (SimCLR/MoCo) for quick sanity checks rather than final models.
