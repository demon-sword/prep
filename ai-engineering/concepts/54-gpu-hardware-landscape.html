GPU Hardware Landscape for AI

The accelerator market in one decision framework — NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell against AMD CDNA, Google TPU, and AWS Trainium — and the memory-capacity arithmetic that rules every hardware choice for LLM serving.

Theory

Why Memory Decides Everything

LLM serving hardware is picked by memory first and FLOPS second. Two numbers dominate: capacity (does the model plus KV cache fit?) and bandwidth (how fast can decode stream weights?). A 70B-parameter model in FP16 needs ~140 GB just for weights — already more than one 80 GB H100 — before a single token of KV cache. And autoregressive decode reads nearly the full weight set per generated token while doing almost no math, so tokens-per-second scales with HBM bandwidth, not tensor FLOPS. Every row in the comparison table below is really a statement about these two numbers, dressed up as a product name.

weights_GB = params x bytesPerParam kvPerToken_B = 2 (K+V) x n_layers x d_model x bytesPerElem [÷ GQA groups] requestKV_GB = kvPerToken_B x contextLen x batch ÷ 1e9 Example: 70B dense, FP16, 80 layers, d=8192 → ~2.6 MB/token; 32k context x 8 concurrent → ~680 GB of KV alone.

That example is why long-context serving is a capacity crisis: at 32k context the KV cache dwarfs the weights, and the hardware answer is either more HBM per GPU (H200/B200, MI300X), KV-cache quantization and eviction, or disaggregated prefill/decode with dedicated decode nodes. Grouped-query attention (GQA) divides the K/V heads and is the main reason modern 70B models are servable at all.

Three non-GPU lines deserve honest summaries. Google TPUs are systolic-array chips with excellent perf-per-dollar for dense transformer math at pod scale, but they rent by the slice on Google Cloud, demand XLA-compiled graphs, and punish dynamic shapes (long-context decode with varying sequence lengths recompiles or pads). AWS Trainium2 (training) and Inferentia2 (inference) win on cost inside AWS with the Neuron SDK, at the price of a narrower kernel library and a portability cliff if you ever leave. Intel Gaudi 3 (128 GB HBM2e) competes on price with an Ethernet-first scale-out story, but third-party kernel and serving-stack support (vLLM backends, fused kernels) trails CUDA by years.

The Interconnect Is Part of the Computer

No frontier model fits on one chip, so multi-GPU fabric is a first-class spec. Inside a node, NVLink (900 GB/s bidirectional per H100 via NVSwitch) makes 8-GPU tensor parallelism feel almost like one big chip; across nodes, InfiniBand NDR (400 Gb/s per port) or AWS EFA carries pipeline and data parallel traffic. Blackwell pushes this further with NVL72 — 72 GPUs in one NVLink domain — explicitly designed so a trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts can tensor-parallelize without touching the slower network. AMD answers with Infinity Fabric, Google with TPU pod interconnect (ICI), but the principle is vendor-neutral: exposed collective time in your nsys trace means the fabric, not the FLOPS, is your computer now.

How to Choose: A Decision Framework

1. Fit the memory first

Weights + worst-case KV must fit the serving tier with headroom. If not: quantize (FP8/FP4, KV quant), shard wider (more TP), or buy capacity (H200, B200, MI300X).

2. Match bandwidth to decode SLOs

Decode tokens/sec ≈ bandwidth ÷ bytes-per-token. H100's 3.3 TB/s vs A100's 2 TB/s is directly ~1.6× decode throughput on the same model.

3. Match compute to prefill/training

Prefill and training are compute-bound: FP8/FP4 throughput and scale-out fabric dominate. Blackwell's FP4 exists for exactly this.

4. Price the software, not the chip

CUDA's moat is the stack: NCCL, cuDNN, CUTLASS, Triton backends, vLLM kernels. Discount rival FLOPS by the kernels you will have to port or wait for.

Two utilization tricks stretch any choice. MIG (multi-instance GPU) slices one A100/H100 into up to 7 isolated instances — perfect for small-model, low-QPS endpoints that would otherwise strand 90% of a chip. And spot/preemptible capacity on last-gen cards (A100, L40S) routinely serves batch and eval workloads at a third of on-demand H100 prices, because evals do not need the newest FLOPS, just cheap correct ones.

Production Reality

In practice the fleet is heterogeneous on purpose: H100/B200 pods for latency-sensitive frontier serving, H200s where 128k+ context blows up KV, MI300X pools where vLLM's ROCm backend is qualified and memory-per-dollar wins, TPUs for the training runs that never leave Google Cloud, and Trainium/Inferentia endpoints for cost-optimized AWS-native inference. The interview-ready sentence is this: pick capacity for fit, bandwidth for decode speed, compute for prefill and training, fabric for scale-out — then discount everything by software maturity, because a chip your kernels do not run on has zero FLOPS.

Key Takeaways

  • Size memory before FLOPS: weights plus worst-case KV cache must fit with headroom, and decode throughput tracks HBM bandwidth (H100 ~3.3 TB/s vs A100 ~2 TB/s) far more than tensor FLOPS — run the capacity arithmetic before shortlisting any card.
  • Blackwell (B200, 192 GB HBM3e, ~8 TB/s, FP4) and H200 (141 GB) exist to defuse the long-context KV crisis, while AMD's MI300X/MI325X counter with 192–256 GB at aggressive memory-per-dollar — but discount rival peaks by ROCm kernel coverage for your exact serving stack (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, NCCL equivalents).
  • TPUs (v5p/Trillium via XLA) and Trainium2/Inferentia2 (via the Neuron SDK) win TCO inside their clouds and punish exit: dynamic-shape decode graphs and portability cliffs are the real cost, so keep cloud-specific silicon on workloads that never need to move.
  • The fabric is part of the computer: NVLink 900 GB/s per GPU and NVL72 keep tensor parallelism on-chip-fast, while InfiniBand NDR 400 Gb/s and EFA carry the rest — exposed collectives in an Nsight Systems trace mean you buy fabric, not FLOPS.
  • Stretch with MIG slices for small endpoints, GQA-aware KV math, FP8/FP4 and KV-cache quantization before buying bigger cards, and last-gen spot (A100, L40S) for batch evals — CUDA's stack maturity still makes NVIDIA the default, challenged per workload, never assumed.
